Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loader Intro and Content Handoff
When the user opens or refreshes the page, the full-screen dark loader first displays Your, Web Experiences, is loading right, and Now together with a continuously advancing count. Each line enters in sequence from below its clipped area, while Now cycles between solid sans-serif and outlined italic styles. Once the intro finishes, the loader fades out and stops covering the main page; the main content enters from below, followed by the brand, section labels, and four-line hero title. The main content then takes over wheel, pointer, and subsequent media interaction, and the page must not remain behind a full-screen overlay while external media is still loading.

### R2 - Hero Title and Flag Tracking
When a desktop user moves the pointer over Works, About, or Contacts, the corresponding section label shifts magnetically toward the pointer within a limited range. After the pointer enters the full Web/ Graphic row, the flag image appears near the pointer's current position and follows it continuously. When either the Web/ or Graphic text is hovered, only that segment changes from solid white text to a white outline and hides its own underline. After the pointer leaves a section label, the text returns to its typographic baseline; after it leaves the Web/ or Graphic text, that segment returns to solid text with its underline; and after it leaves the full row, the flag fades out. Rapid repeated entries and exits must reflect only the current hover state, without accumulating tracking objects or leaving the flag at stale coordinates where it covers the showreel.

### R3 - Vertical Narrative and Parallax Order
As the user scrolls down from the hero, the page proceeds vertically through the showreel, OUR PROJECTS, About Obys, the marquee keyword section, and the Let's Create footer in that order. Each section continuously enters and exits along its own parallax direction, and reverse scrolling returns through the established content order in the opposite direction. When the user stops after a fast scroll or immediately scrolls back, the visible boundaries and interactive areas of titles, project media, the video control, the awards panel, and contact content remain aligned. Sections must not snap back to stale coordinates, exchange their semantic order, leave an entire blank span, or overflow horizontally.

### R4 - Showreel Playback State
When a desktop user moves the pointer within the showreel preview, the global difference-blend cursor fades out and the orange circular control updates its position to follow the pointer within that area. Once the video resource is playable, clicking reveals the same video and requests playback, changes the icon to pause, and shrinks the circular control. Clicking again pauses the media, hides the video layer, and restores the preview, play icon, and control size. After the pointer leaves, the global cursor reappears and the orange control stops following it, but the exit itself does not change the media state. Repeated interaction after returning still acts on the current video; when playable, the media continues according to its loop setting after reaching the end, while scrolling, project browsing, and text reading remain available if the resource is not yet ready.

### R5 - Project Grid and Media Pairings
After the user scrolls into OUR PROJECTS, the section must show its title, section marker, divider, four staggered project cards, and circular arrow objects. In order, the cards contain the paired images of the Olga Prudka wordmark and a yellow-toned figure, an Ochi presentation card and yellow-green eyes, a silver sports car and its cockpit, and a We Are Laxer portrait and pink-and-white still life. Each pair displays its first image by default. When a desktop pointer enters a card, only that card locally reveals the paired second image around the pointer position, and it returns to the first image after the pointer leaves. Each card must retain its own pairing, crop, and container boundary during scrolling, hovering, and viewport reflow. On desktop, the first card is a smaller portrait format and the remaining cards use a larger portrait format; at viewport widths of 600px or less, they become an equal-width single column, and the fluid imagery must not cover the title.

### R6 - Local Fluid Reveal on Project Cards
When a desktop user moves the pointer within any project card, the paired second image forms a local fluid reveal around the current pointer position, with edges that keep changing. The reveal center follows the pointer and always remains clipped to the card boundary, while the locally visible area, transition between the two images, and edge shape unfold continuously as one composition. During a steady hover, the image remains coherent and readable. After the pointer exits, the effect continuously contracts from the final pointer position and restores the first image. If the pointer leaves midway, repeatedly re-enters, or changes direction, the change must continue naturally from the then-current image and use the latest pointer position. Once the pointer has fully left, no local reveal may remain persistently visible, and no other card may be affected.

### R7 - Independent Project Card States
When the user moves from a project card with an established local reveal into another card, the departing card begins continuously contracting toward its first image as soon as the pointer crosses its boundary, while the new card starts responding around its new internal pointer position. At the handoff, the two cards may respectively be in exit and entry transitions, but their pointer positions, visible areas, and transition progress remain independent. As the user moves among multiple cards, into blank space, or back into a card, the card under the pointer updates its own hover image while all other cards continue their own recovery processes. After the pointer leaves, every card independently finishes contracting and returns to its first-image idle state.

### R8 - Circular Project Prompt Reveal
When a desktop user hovers over either circular arrow object in the project grid, the white inner circle expands outward from the center and covers the arrow, after which the welcome message transitions from a scaled-down, transparent state to a readable state. If the pointer leaves partway through the expansion, the white inner circle immediately contracts toward the center, while the message shrinks with its container and fades out through its own transition, ultimately revealing the arrow again. During rapid repeated entries or a move from one circular object to the other, each object retains only its own current progress. The text must not appear on its own before the white inner circle, remain above the project cards after the collapse finishes, or leave the arrow away from the center of the circle.

### R9 - About Content and Award Associations
After the user scrolls to About Obys, the content is read in the order of the large title, agency philosophy, agency image, collaboration copy, and blue awards panel. Within the panel, each of Awwwards, FWA, CSSDA, Behance, and The Very Best Of remains associated one-to-one with its current count and same-row description, using the same set of current content throughout. On desktop, the image and collaboration copy sit side by side, and the blue panel overlaps the lower-right area of the image. At viewport widths of 600px or less, these objects are arranged vertically in sequence and the panel no longer overlaps. While the media is still loading, the philosophy, award text, and contact meaning remain readable.

### R10 - Keyword Marquees and Footer Letterforms
As the user scrolls through the keyword section, the repeated text bands containing Sport, Fasion, Technology and Science, partners, Travel loop continuously. Adjacent rows retain opposite directions, repeated copies join end to end at the boundary, and each italic serif emphasized word always moves with the phrase to which it belongs. When a desktop user reaches the footer and hovers over Let's Create, the solid sans-serif characters fade out in sequence, after which the outlined italic characters appear in sequence; the sequence reverses after the pointer leaves. Reverse scrolling and repeated hovering must not alter the marquees' inherent directions, leave the two title versions overlapped for an extended period, or move the contact columns below.

### R11 - Narrow-Screen Single-Column Layout
When the page opens at a viewport width of 600px or less, the top Works, About, and Contacts labels are hidden and the brand graphic remains fixed at the upper right. The hero title is scaled down and left-aligned, the showreel fills the available width with the orange control centered over the media, the project cards become an equal-width single column, and the desktop circular arrow objects leave the layout. The About image, collaboration copy, blue panel, and the Socials, Address, and Say Hi! contact columns unfold vertically in order. Every object remains within the viewport width without horizontal scrolling. At wider viewport widths, the page uses the offset grid, staggered project cards, overlapping blue panel, and horizontal contact columns.

### R12 - Media Loading and Page Reinitialization
While the user waits for remote media such as the flag, showreel preview and video, or About image, the loader intro completes according to its own choreography and hands control to the main content. The explicitly sized showreel placeholder remains in the layout, while local project images, page titles, award text, and contact information remain readable. The flag and About image appear at their designated positions after their respective media load successfully. After the user refreshes the page, the loader, scrolling, and applicable pointer interactions are reestablished; the video layer returns to its transparent idle state, while the flag, local project-card reveals, circular white inner layers, and alternate Let's Create letterforms all return to their default states. The remote media restart their respective loading processes and appear when ready.
