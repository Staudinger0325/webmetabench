Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Hero Branding and Media Entry Points
When the user opens the page and waits for the hero media to become playable, they should see a muted, looping real-estate video filling the viewport; the Magma logo at the top; the Book a Demo and menu buttons; and Experience Real Estate Agility, the introductory copy, and Learn More at the lower left. Before scrolling begins, these layers maintain a clear front-to-back order. After the user resizes the desktop viewport, the video continues to use cover cropping, and the media does not push the title or navigation out of the container. While the media is still loading, the existing text, buttons, and hero layout remain visible.

### R2 - Scroll-Synchronized Highlighting in the DTT Explanations
When the user scrolls down from the hero into the CERTIFY YOUR BUILDING AS A DIGITAL TWIN TOKEN (DTT), CONNECT THE BUILDING'S TWIN TO YOUR STAKEHOLDERS, and INCREASE YOUR DIGITAL TWIN TOKEN'S VALUE explanations, each eyebrow heading remains white, while the long body copy changes one character at a time from dim blue-gray to white in reading order without disrupting the content structure formed by punctuation, spaces, and line breaks. When the user pauses, scrolls back a short distance, and then continues forward, the highlighting progress stays synchronized with the current scroll position and holds at the current position while paused. Each of the three body-copy sequences uses its own state and does not change a later explanation before the user enters it.

### R3 - Transitions Between Pinned Canvas Sections
As the user scrolls in sequence through the DTT certification, stakeholder, and DTT value explanations and enters the full-screen canvas following each one, the current canvas remains pinned to the viewport for its own presentation interval, with continued scrolling driving that section's content. It is released only after the interval ends, allowing the next text section to continue from below. When the user crosses a boundary quickly, returns to the preceding section, and enters the canvas again, the pinned or released state remains determined by the current section. The preceding and following text does not cover the canvas prematurely, and the preceding canvas does not linger in the next section's layout.

### R4 - Continuous State Across the Canvas Narratives
As the user scrolls through any pinned canvas interval, resumes after pausing, reviews content within the section, and passes through the interval again, the assembly and rotation of the building components, the people and viewpoint in the circular interior, and the third visual sequence continue through adjacent frames according to the current reading progress. When scrolling stops, each sequence holds the state corresponding to the current position. The three sequences each retain their own media and progress state; in-section review and repeated passes continue from frames adjacent to the current position, and the preceding section's frame state, crop position, and loading result do not carry over into the following section.

### R5 - Product Narrative and Section Order
When the user scrolls continuously from the hero to the bottom of the page, they should read, in order, the DTT certification, stakeholder connection, increase in DTT value, Magma DTT, What is Magma ?, company capabilities, Recent Blog, Feature In, and conversion-footer sections. The meaning of the body copy and its corresponding media always remain at the same point in the narrative. During review of any section and repeated scrolling across a section boundary, the content order, background-color hierarchy, and heading associations remain unchanged. The same explanation is never paired with an unrelated canvas, photograph, or video.

### R6 - Brand Video and Company Explanations
When the user leaves the third canvas and enters the Magma DTT section, they should see a muted, looping, glass-effect video covering the full viewport, with Magma DTT and LEARN MORE overlaid at the bottom center. Continuing to scroll reveals the two-column title-and-image composition for What is Magma ? followed by three groups of company explanations. When the user scrolls away and returns, and when the media is becoming playable again, the overlay positions of the heading and CTA do not change. Web3 platform, The Digital Twin Token: The building’s unique digital copy, and An Index of digitization maintain their fixed reading order and remain separate paragraphs.

### R7 - Blog and Media Content Correspondence
When the user scrolls into Recent Blog, the thumbnail, category-and-date text, and article title for every visible entry come from the same current content record and are arranged vertically in the order defined by the page, with View all after the list. Continued scrolling then reaches the Feature In heading and endorsement copy. After a current content record changes or the page reloads, its image, metadata, and title remain synchronized as a group and do not retain text or media from any other entry. Feature In does not display media logos that the page does not provide.

### R8 - Narrow-Screen Content Reflow
When the user resizes the viewport from desktop width to medium-narrow and mobile widths, the navigation logo and buttons, hero title and introduction, explanatory body copy, company-capabilities section, and footer social rows resize and reflow according to their corresponding narrow-screen styles. The empty left column of the company explanation is hidden, and the three groups of text on the right expand to the available width. When the user restores a wider viewport, the layout is recalculated for the current width while preserving the original content order and any value entered in E-mail. The canvases, videos, and cards remain constrained by their respective containers and do not create a separate horizontal browsing flow.

### R9 - Footer Social-Row Hover Feedback
When the user moves the pointer over the Twitter, linkedIn, Instagram, Telegram, and Youtube rows in sequence, the current row's blue fill layer expands continuously from its middle to fill the entire row. The name and up-right arrow remain above the fill layer at all times, while the other rows retain their dark backgrounds. When the pointer leaves the current row, moves quickly to the next row, and repeatedly enters the same row, the preceding fill retracts and only the currently hovered row is active. The hover layer does not cover the Become an early adopter section or the bottom navigation layout.

### R10 - Session State of the Subscription Input
When the user scrolls to the bottom of the footer, clicks the single-line input whose placeholder text is E-mail, and types ordinary text, the input gains focus and displays the value entered during this interaction. Its transparent background, bottom border, and Subscribe heading remain distinguishable, and the entered content does not overlap the placeholder text. After focus moves elsewhere, the viewport is resized, and the user returns to the input area, the value is retained within the current page session. This area has no submission control, so typing does not create undefined validation, loading, or completion states.

### R11 - Redrawing and Resource Lifecycle
When the user changes the window width and height at any point in a canvas sequence, the pixel dimensions of all three canvases synchronize with the new viewport, and each canvas redraws its own current state using the new cover scale and centered offset. The scroll activation positions are then updated to reflect the reflowed page. While locally hosted frames, remote frames, videos, or images are still loading and the user continues to scroll, the available text and established section order remain browsable. Once a resource is ready, its media updates only within the designated container, without resetting the current reading progress or pushing neighboring sections out of their intended order.
