Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Entering the Scene and First-Person Movement
When the user opens the game, the FishingF'reel onboarding card, quest tracker, top progression information, crosshair, and control instructions must appear over a full-viewport lake scene. After the user clicks the scene, the onboarding card must close and the pointer must be captured for scene control; moving the mouse changes the camera's orientation, while W, A, S, and D move the player along the current viewing direction across the dock and shore terrain. Camera height must continuously follow the deck and terrain, and the deep-water boundary must prevent further movement. After the pointer is released, the current position and quest state must remain unchanged and the page must display a click-to-resume prompt; clicking again must restore camera input.

### R2 - Lake Time and Water-Surface Visuals
After the user enters the scene and waits as in-game time advances, the sky, sun direction, key light, ambient light, fog color, background color, and water-surface highlights must change in sync. The water's normal texture must continuously produce fine ripples and reflect the sky and shoreline; when the user turns the view, these lighting relationships must continue to reference the same sun position. When the player equips or unequips glasses or a hat with polarization, water-surface glare, disturbance, and the full-screen color treatment must update with the current loadout so that fish visibility remains consistent with the equipped state, without changing fish positions or the fishing process.

### R3 - Cast Charging and Tackle Coordination
When the user holds the left mouse button or Space while idle, the page must enter the aiming state, the CAST POWER bar must change continuously according to the current Hold or Timing mode, and the fishing rod must bend backward in sync. On release, the rod must complete its backward load, forward whip, and decaying rebound, while the bobber travels along a parabolic path determined by the camera direction, charge level, and current rod capability until it reaches the actual surface. The fishing line must run from the reel through the guides and rod tip to the bobber, and the waiting state must not begin before the flight is complete. A landing on shore terrain must display On dry land — reel it in and remain retrievable; only a landing on the water surface may enable the floating bobber and fish-bite logic.

### R4 - Waiting, Lure Action, and Full Retrieval
After the bobber enters the water, waiting must show the bobber moving slightly up and down, ring ripples periodically expanding and fading, and the slack fishing line forming a sagging arc. Pressing the right mouse button or E must briefly intensify the lure action, make the bobber oscillate quickly over a small range, and then let that action decay gradually. When the user holds the left mouse button or Space, or scrolls upward after scroll-wheel reeling has been enabled, the line length must shorten continuously, the reel must rotate, and the taut-line constraint must draw the bobber toward the rod tip. Once the line is fully retrieved, the bobber and paid-out line segment must be hidden, prompts and temporary state must be cleared, and the game must return to idle.

### R5 - Splash and Ripple Feedback
When the user completes and releases a short cast onto the water beside the dock, the point where the bobber contacts the water must simultaneously trigger a splash sound, a ring ripple that expands and fades from the landing point, and a crown of droplets radiating outward from around that point. Each droplet must independently rise along a parabolic path, pass its own apex, and fall back to the water. Throughout each droplet's ascent, passage over its apex, and descent, its horizontal trajectory must continue in its own radial direction away from the center of the landing point, and the splash crown must remain radially spread. Each droplet must be hidden according to its own lifecycle after returning to the water. Ordinary water entries at different charge levels and at near or far distances must follow the same trajectory rule, and repeated casts must generate only the new feedback associated with the current cast's landing point.

### R6 - Fish Response and Hook-Set Window
When the user casts valid bait into the water and waits or twitches the lure, the fish in the current body of water must continue cruising, while matching species turn toward and approach the bobber according to distance, bait, time of day, and lure action. Once a fish commits to a bite, the bobber must be pulled underwater intermittently and FISH ON! and the hook-set prompt must become responsive at the same time. Pressing the right mouse button or E while the prompt is active must start the fight and bind the current fish to it. If the user does not act before the window expires, the fish must disengage, the prompt must close, and the game must return to waiting. Only one fish may occupy the bite or fight state at any time.

### R7 - Fish-Fighting Tension and Outcome Branches
After setting the hook, the user holds the right mouse button to load the rod, holds the left mouse button to reel, and releases the inputs while the fish sprints or jumps. The fish must evolve according to its species through swimming, sprinting, diving, head-shaking, jumping, and fatigue states, while the LINE, ROD, TIRED, and FISH IN readings remain synchronized frame by frame with the rod's bend, the fishing line's arc, the reel, and the fish's position. Tension exceeding the line's capacity, exhaustion of the reel's line capacity, or sustained heavy pulling during a jump must each lead to the corresponding escape outcome and clear the session objects. Bringing the fish close to shore must open the catch card and record the species, size, earnings, and experience from that catch; pressing Space or the left mouse button must then return the game to an idle state in which another cast can be made.

### R8 - Fish-Fighting Tension Readout and Color-Band Mapping
After a fish is hooked and the fight begins, when the player changes line tension by lifting the rod, reeling, letting line out, or waiting, the fill width in the LINE row must update continuously in proportion to the current tension divided by the strength of the equipped line. Whether the fill is short, medium, or nearly full, its visible area must contain the complete same gradient from teal through gold to red, with the entire gradient compressed or stretched together with the visible width. As tension rises and falls, only the fill length may change; individual colors must not be progressively revealed or hidden at fixed coordinates along the full track. Width changes must use a 120-millisecond linear transition. Above 85% of the line's strength, the existing danger flash must continue to trigger, while the track dimensions, corner radius, LINE value, and the state relationships among ROD, TIRED, and FISH IN remain unchanged.

### R9 - Quest Progression and Local Continuity
As the user clicks the scene, moves, casts, sets the hook, works the lure, lands a fish, or completes equipment and travel actions in sequence, only a matching incomplete objective in the current quest may gain progress. A completed objective must receive a check mark, and the reward for the entire quest must be settled only once before the game switches to the next quest. Catch settlement must update experience, money, the species collection, catch records, and quest state in sync. After the page is refreshed or reopened, the level and carried-over experience, money, equipment, loadout, consumables, areas, Angler's Log, quests, and settings must be restored from local data, while session state such as the bobber, current fish, tension, and catch card must return to a safe starting point.

### R10 - Tackle Box, Shop, and Loadout
During an allowed fishing state, when the user presses I, B, or Tab to open the panel and then clicks TACKLE BOX, SHOP, or an equipment category, the pointer must leave scene control and the player's footsteps must stop. The item grid, selected card, details area, action button, and bottom loadout bar must all remain aligned to the same selection. Clicking the close button or background, or using the shortcut, must return to the scene. Equipped, owned, level-locked, unaffordable, and purchasable states must each provide the corresponding action availability. After a purchase or equipment change succeeds, money, inventory, consumables, the top equipment summary, and loadout values for cast distance, line strength, drag, attraction, and polarization must update in sync.

### R11 - Map Travel and Angler's Log
When the user presses M to open MAP · FAST TRAVEL, the Stillwater Pond, Cedar Lake, and Granite Rapids nodes must respectively show whether they are the current location, unlocked, or locked. Travel here may be clicked only for a location that is unlocked in the save and is not the current location. Completing travel must close the map, and reopening it must show the updated current location and data for the current body of water. When the user presses L to open ANGLER'S LOG, discovered fish species must display their real names and cumulative records, while undiscovered entries must display ??? and undiscovered. The groups for each body of water and the summary data must remain consistent with the current catch collection. Closing either panel must not alter fishing progression state.

### R12 - Settings Response and Viewport Lifecycle
During an allowed fishing state, pressing Esc must open SETTINGS. After the user adjusts the Look Sensitivity or Field of View slider, or changes Invert Look Y, Cast Meter, Reel Input, Reel with Scroll Wheel, or Sound, the camera, input mapping, prompts, and audio must immediately use the current settings. Clicking Reset to Defaults must restore both the controls and actual behavior to their initial values. When the user resizes the browser, closes and reopens an overlay, or releases and recaptures the pointer, the canvas must continue to fill the viewport, the camera projection must update for the new aspect ratio, and panels must remain within the visible area and use internal scrolling. The page must not gain an outer scrolling region or clear the current progression data or loadout state.
