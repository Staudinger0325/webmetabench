Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loading Screen and World Entry
When the user opens the root page, the main viewport first presents resource progress against a deep-blue radial background. The text follows the "Loading [current progress]%" format and updates continuously as models, textures, fonts, and product sections actually arrive. After loading completes, it gives way to the 3D canvas and the "WASD - Move" and "Shift - Sprint" control prompts appear at the bottom. During staged loading, the character, grass, paths, and portal that have already appeared remain in place and operable; later heavy content for Skills, About, Game, Projects, and Lamps is added at its fixed world coordinates. It must not shift existing objects, create document scrolling, or display incomplete progress as complete. If a resource request fails, a clear loading state remains so the user can refresh and recover.

### R2 - Character Speed, Input, and Follow Camera
From the spawn point, while the user continuously holds W or the Up Arrow key, the character moves forward normally toward a target speed of 5 world units per second and remains in Walk. While directional input is still active, holding Shift changes the target speed to 20 world units per second, four times the normal speed, and enters Run. Shift+D first normalizes the direction and then moves laterally at the same sprint speed; releasing Shift returns to the normal target speed. Continuous acceleration brings the speed progressively closer to its target, and combined directions do not add extra speed. After one direction is released, only movement on that axis decays; after all directions are released, the character stops at the current location. The character's orientation and the rear-overhead camera continue to follow the actual velocity. Switching between normal movement and sprinting causes no position jump, and maintaining the same speed mode does not repeatedly restart the action from its first frame.

### R3 - Character Animation and World Motion
When the user changes from standing still to normal movement, then holds Shift to sprint and releases the controls in sequence, the character action changes through Idle, Walk, Run, and then the reverse order. Every change uses a brief cross-fade, and a continuously active action does not repeatedly restart from its first frame. While the user waits in place, the streetlamp lighting, the orientation of the skill sprites, the swinging "Welcome!" text in About, and the scene materials continue to update at their respective rhythms. Frame-rate or display-density adjustments may change only the rendering load; the character's world position, the spatial relationship between the roads and trees, the orientation of the area text, and the boundaries of the sensor carpets retain the same meaning.

### R4 - Spatial Content Map
As the user moves along the stone-slab paths and signposts, they can find the Skills icon array, About's Education and Links, five Projects booths, the Game track with its doll, and the Contact portal within the same grassy world. Large 3D headings, models, and local lighting establish a recognizable directional hierarchy for each area. Walking backward, approaching from the side, or traveling back and forth between areas does not change the fixed positions of these objects. The skill icons continue to face the camera, each project's main and secondary models remain grouped, and the signposts provide directions without replacing movement through the world.

### R5 - Skills, Education, and Personal Links
When the user enters and walks around the Skills and About areas, the React, Node, MongoDB, Python, SQL, C++, HTML, CSS, Three.js, Bootstrap, Next.js, and Flutter icons remain front-facing and readable. Education shows the established schools, major, years, and location, while Links shows Hackerrank, Codechef, Leetcode, Linkedin, and Github. Entering the sensor zone beside any platform name displays a black information plaque containing only the "Visit (Press V)" prompt, and pressing V opens the current platform. Leaving the zone removes both the plaque and the visit shortcut. Entering another platform then uses only the new platform's target and does not display the "Github (Press G)" prompt.

### R6 - Project Booths and Plaques in 3D Space
When the user steps onto the red sensor carpet for IIC DCRUST, Museum Counsel, Social Freaks, Tic Tac Toe, or Chat App, a translucent black information plaque appears and shows the current project's title, description, "Visit (Press V)", "Github (Press G)", and "Move out of Plate to Exit". Pressing V or G opens the corresponding target shown on the plaque in a new browsing context. As the character continues moving within the carpet, the plaque's position, size, and perspective tilt update with the camera matrix on every frame, keeping the plaque anchored to the corresponding booth in the 3D scene instead of fixed to the viewport. Once the character crosses out of the current carpet, the plaque disappears immediately and the V/G shortcuts simultaneously lose their scope for that project. Pressing them on open ground does not open the previous targets, and stepping onto another carpet activates only the new project's copy and links. Repeatedly entering and leaving does not accumulate old shortcut behavior or cause duplicate openings.

### R7 - External Visits and Portal Navigation
When the user triggers a visit action while a valid project or personal-link plaque is active, the external content opens in a separate browsing context and the portfolio's controlled movement keys are released at the same time. On returning to the main world, the character remains at the location where it was left. Entering the sensor zone inside the Contact portal instead opens the contact page with its message model and form. After leaving a sensor zone, closing the external page, returning, or refreshing, the main world retains only the current character and current area state: the character does not continue moving because of previously held direction keys, and a closed plaque no longer responds to visit shortcuts.

### R8 - Portal and Environmental Materials
When the user approaches the Contact area and waits without moving, time-driven noise distortion continuously creates a flowing texture between white and purple across the portal surface. The portal-frame lights, local point light, baked grass material, and streetlamp materials together maintain the nighttime spatial depth, while the "Contact" heading and entrance prompt remain anchored. After the user moves the camera, adjusts the viewport, or returns from Contact, the flow continues updating from the current time and the portal surface does not become a static solid color. The explanatory text, models, and sensor entrance remain aligned with the same portal, and repeated entry triggers only one navigation for the current entry.

### R9 - Movement Penalty During the Doll's Turn
After entering the Game start area, the user holds W to advance toward the finish while the doll faces away from the character and keeps holding it as the doll turns to face the character. As soon as the turn is complete, if the character is still moving, the round ends immediately and displays "Game Over! Try Again." Continuous movement caused by A, S, D, or the arrow keys follows the same rule. The decision is based on the character's actual movement state throughout the period when the doll faces the character, regardless of whether the key was pressed before or after the turn. Releasing and pressing again, holding continuously, pressing combinations, and keyboard auto-repeat cannot bypass this state rule.

### R10 - Game Start, Completion, and Reset
After the user enters the start sensor zone, the "Start Game" sign changes to an in-progress state and the doll continuously alternates between facing away and facing the character. If the user advances with the forward or backward controls only during the facing-away periods and enters the finish sensor zone, the current turning cycle ends, "Congratulations!" appears beside the finish, and no further movement penalties are applied in that round. A failure or completion message remains briefly, then clears and restores the ready-to-start sign. Returning to the start can begin one new round. Repeatedly stepping into the start zone cannot create multiple concurrent turning sequences, and the previous round's completion, failure, and timing state does not carry into the next round.

### R11 - Contact Page Model and Form Flow
After the user enters the Contact page, the message model on the left continuously spins and moves along a periodic path across all three axes, while horizontal mouse movement makes the model's orientation follow the current pointer. The "Contact Me" area on the right accepts four required entries: Name, Email, Subject, and Message. Clicking Submit disables the button while the request is pending. When the service returns a successful result, the page displays "Message Sent.", clears all four entries at the same time, and re-enables the button. When the service returns a failed result, the page displays the service's explanation, preserves the entered content, and re-enables the button. After leaving and entering again, only one model animation loop and one mouse response remain active.

### R12 - Viewport Adaptation and Rebuild Cleanup
When the user changes the desktop window's width or height or the device pixel density, the 3D canvas and bottom control bar remain together within the visible window. The canvas pixel dimensions adapt to the rendering load, while the world coordinates, the two movement speed tiers, the camera-follow direction, the HTML information-plaque anchors, and the sensor boundaries remain unchanged. The Contact page continues to keep the model area and form area side by side, with the fields remaining in the form column. After the user travels back and forth between the main world and Contact, refreshes, or unloads and rebuilds the page, previous keyboard and mouse responses, animation cycles, sensor-zone behavior, and graphical resources are released, and the new page establishes only a single active instance. Project plaques that were already exited and game states that already ended do not return.
