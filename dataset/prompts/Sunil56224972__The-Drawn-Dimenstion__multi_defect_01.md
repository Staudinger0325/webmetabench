Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loading and Entrance Reveal
When the user opens the experience for the first time and waits for resources to become ready, a full-viewport paper loading layer covers the canvas. Its progress indicator advances with the actual readiness of the textures, fonts, 3D scene, and themed spaces, and entrance objects do not receive accidental input while the loading layer is present. Once the key resources have finished preloading, the paper exits through an opening or retracting motion to reveal an entrance with the PORTFOLIO sign, technology stickers on the double doors, and courtyard props. If resources load slowly, the loading state remains visible rather than revealing a scene that is missing major objects.

### R2 - Entrance Doors and Entry Camera
At the entrance, when the user moves the pointer over the double doors and clicks an active area, the door panels and handles first indicate that the doors can be entered through partial color reveal and pose changes. The double doors then open, and the camera moves continuously through the doorway into the corridor while courtyard objects leave the primary viewing area as the camera advances. Until entry finishes, repeated clicks neither create another door animation or camera path nor expose the corridor controls early. Once entry is complete, the entrance prompt gives way to the exploration prompt, the entrance doors leave the primary interaction layer, and the MAP, sound, and ACHIEVEMENTS controls appear in accordance with the entered state.

### R3 - Cat Pointer Gaze
When the user moves the mouse in the entrance courtyard, moving the desktop pointer from the center of the view toward the left, right, upward, or downward, both of the cat's pupils smoothly shift together toward the corresponding horizontal position and follow the vertical position with a smaller range of motion. When the pointer returns to the center, the pupils continuously return to a neutral, forward-facing gaze. This dynamic response always remains within the eye sockets and affects only the pupils; it does not move the cat's body, courtyard props, entrance double doors, or camera. A touch environment retains the neutral gaze while still allowing entry, and repeated pointer movements leave no residual offset.

### R4 - Continuous Corridor Exploration
After entering the corridor, when the user moves forward or backward with the mouse wheel, trackpad, or an equivalent touch gesture, the camera moves continuously through the depth of the hand-drawn corridor. After input stops, its position settles with controlled easing, while the walls, floor, light fixtures, graffiti, and room doors remain continuously connected across section boundaries. Moving the pointer provides limited horizontal and vertical parallax. While room entry, paper teleportation, or an exit animation takes control of the camera, corridor scrolling and parallax pause; once control is returned, exploration continues from the current landing position without jumping back, and the characters and adjacent corridor sections retain the same spatial relationships.

### R5 - Room-Door Hover and Entry State
When the user approaches and hovers over any About, Gallery, Studio, or Contact room door in the corridor, the current door's drawing layer gradually reveals color, its handle rotates slightly in the opening direction, and its panel simultaneously takes on a partially pushed-open pose. If the pointer briefly leaves and immediately re-enters the same door leaf, the new hover state continues to maintain the door panel, handle, and partially open pose together; an unfinished fade-out from the previous pointer leave must not hide the handle independently. After the pointer fully leaves, the handle rotates back, the panel closes, and the color retracts through the reverse process. On click, the selected door opens and the camera enters the themed space matching its sign; during entry, other doors and repeated clicks cannot change the target.

### R6 - Side-Door Camera Guidance
As the user moves forward or backward through the corridor and approaches any side door, the camera view smoothly turns away from the forward direction toward the side containing that door. The turn gradually increases while nearing the door, then continuously returns to the corridor's forward direction after passing beyond its perceptible range, in step with the camera's movement at the current position. This guidance coexists with scrolling movement, pointer parallax, and door clicking, and must not lock the camera in front of the door. When the user passes doors on different sides in succession, the viewing direction transitions smoothly according to the nearest eligible door, while the door models, door signs, and corridor content remain explorable.

### R7 - Room Return Position
When the user clicks a room door from an actual position in the corridor to enter a themed space and then clicks the back arrow in the upper-left corner, any open content card exits first. The camera then follows the exit sequence back to the same position and orientation recorded before entry, after which corridor input and the ability to enter that door again are restored. If the user enters through the same room door from another position within its clickable range and then returns, the landing position changes according to the position before this entry and must not be collapsed to a single preset camera point for that room. No room-specific input or overlay content remains after the exit.

### R8 - Map-Targeted Teleportation
When the user opens the MAP paper panel and selects a named room region other than the current location, the panel closes and a paper mask closes over the canvas. Only after the mask has closed are the camera position change and the target room-door entry completed, and the paper reopens after the target content is ready. When opening finishes, the visible room, back arrow, current-room state, map highlight, and subsequent exit path all correspond to the target selected by this click. During teleportation, scrolling, returning, room-door clicks, and repeated selections cannot overwrite the target, and selecting the current location does not start the paper closing and opening sequence.

### R9 - Map and Global Controls
When the user opens MAP from the corridor or a themed space, the paper panel presents named regions for About, Gallery, Studio, and Contact together with a marker for the current location. Hovering over a region or focusing it with the keyboard displays its colored preview; after leaving it, the preview retracts unless it represents the current location. Once the panel opens, focus moves into its operable elements. Escape, the close button, and an interaction outside the panel can all close it. After it closes, its regions no longer receive focus or clicks, and other panels such as sound and achievements do not overlap the map's primary close control.

### R10 - Themed-Space Content and Single Selection
When the user scrolls in ABOUT, the paper airplane, clouds, sky sections, and experience nodes advance continuously along the flight path. Upon reaching SKILLS, hovering over a balloon advances it from line art to color, and clicking it completes the full cycle of bursting, displaying its name, fading the text, and floating back up from below; repeated clicks can trigger the full cycle again. In THE GALLERY, if the user clicks one project card and then quickly clicks another before the first card's opening process finishes, only the first active display sequence may continue: the second card must not flip concurrently or leave an isolated card behind. After that process finishes, switching projects first retracts the current details before opening new content, and device selection in THE STUDIO likewise maintains exactly one current object.

### R11 - Contact, Sound, and Achievements
After the user enters CONTACT, the social barrels continuously float with different phases and partially reveal color on hover, and clicking a barrel opens only its corresponding destination. As the user fills in the paper message form, the content and focus of Email, Subject, and Message are presented in sync, and long text wraps within the usable width of the paper. Before submission, if required content is missing or the email format is invalid, the input remains and a corresponding prompt appears; while submission is in progress, repeated submission cannot be triggered. Mute and volume settings remain consistent between rooms, and the ACHIEVEMENTS panel displays obtained and unobtained states according to the actual exploration record.

### R12 - Touch Adaptation and Reinitialization
When the user resizes the desktop viewport, rotates a touch device, or continues exploring on a narrow screen, the canvas dimensions and camera projection update together, while MAP, the back arrow, prompt strip, and content cards remain within reachable areas. Touch mode can complete the entrance, corridor, MAP, room, project, and form flows without relying on hover. When the page resumes from the background, the canvas is rebuilt, or the user leaves the experience and enters it again, old wheel, pointer, keyboard, and resize listeners and unfinished animations are released, so one input drives only one response in the current scene.
