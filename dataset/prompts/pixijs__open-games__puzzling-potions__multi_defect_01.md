Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loading, Home Screen, and Entry Transitions
When the user opens the page and waits for resources to become ready, the screen presents, in order, the animated cauldron, "Game Brewing," "We’re Ready!," and purple smoke clouds that spread downward to cover the view. It then reveals the home screen with the Puzzling Potions logo, an idling dragon, "Start Game," the settings control, and two external links. After the user presses the primary button, the home-screen objects exit and the bookshelf drop-in and opening cloud-message sequence begins. During loading and scene transitions, the view remains fully covered and destination controls remain disabled; home-screen buttons become available only after their reveal finishes. Repeated clicks must not stack multiple game screens, and when the user returns or re-enters, the shared background, corresponding music, and current screen objects remain consistent.

### R2 - Settings, Pausing, and Persistent State
When the user opens Settings from the home screen or results screen, drags the "Master Volume," "BGM Volume," and "SFX Volume" sliders, selects Easy Mode, Normal Mode, or Hard Mode, and then presses "OK" to return, the audio levels update immediately with the sliders, exactly one difficulty remains highlighted, and the overlay and background blur fully clear. Reopening Settings or refreshing the page restores the saved slider positions and difficulty. When Settings is opened on the game screen, the timer, piece movement, and pending board-resolution sequence pause; all three sliders remain adjustable, the difficulty group is dimmed and read-only, and closing Settings resumes the same board from the same point in its progress.

### R3 - Opening Sequence and Initial Board
After the user presses "Start Game" and waits for the entrance sequence, the bookshelf board, with 9 rows, 7 columns, and 50-pixel cells, first drops into the center. "Ready?" and "GO!" then play in sequence, followed by the completed reveal of the cauldron, score, and pause control. The 60-second timer starts and dragging becomes available only after every preparation animation has finished. The initial grid contains only the regular creature icons for the current difficulty and has no pre-existing horizontal or vertical three-piece match. After the user presses "Play again" on the results screen, the old board, score, combo, pending resolution sequence, and time all reset to the starting state of a new game.

### R4 - Difficulty and Regular-Piece Pools
When the user selects Easy Mode, Normal Mode, and Hard Mode in turn in an editable Settings panel, closes the panel, and starts a new game for each selection, the regular-piece pool grows with the difficulty: Easy uses the four types dragon, frog, newt, and snake; Normal adds spider for a total of five; and Hard adds yeti for a total of six. The initial fill and every later refill from the top use the same piece pool selected for that game. Refreshing the page and replaying from the results screen continue to use the saved difficulty. Difficulty remains read-only during a game, and the four special-piece types are created only by matching patterns rather than being mixed directly into the random regular-piece pool.

### R5 - Drag Mapping and Input During Board Resolution
When the user presses and holds a piece, drags horizontally, vertically, or diagonally beyond the activation distance, and releases, the game selects the adjacent cell along the axis with the greater displacement. A valid swap remains in its new position, while an invalid swap completes its outward motion and return. An out-of-bounds drag, an external release, or a touch cancellation clears the gesture without creating an out-of-bounds cell. During match bursts, afterimage flights, gravity-driven falls, and refills from the top, moving pieces remain individually locked while other settled pieces continue to accept valid swaps or outward-and-return attempts. Repeatedly dragging a locked piece does not stack movement, and each new gesture applies to the board state at that moment rather than waiting for every animation to finish.

### R6 - Falling, Refilling, and Cascade Timing
When the user creates a clear by dragging and waits for automatic resolution, the matched pieces finish clearing first, existing pieces in each affected column fall into the lowest empty cells, and new pieces enter simultaneously from above the board, stacked by column. The next round of match bursts, displayed-score catch-up, and combo-multiplier updates begins only after every existing and newly added piece has arrived at and aligned with a bookshelf cell. If the settled grid still contains a match, the same order repeats. Resolution is complete only when the board is full again, every piece is settled, and "Combo!" and the multiplier retract together. If the game is paused partway through, resolution stops at its current point, and resuming does not skip any unfinished falling or settling animation.

### R7 - Special-Piece Formation Patterns
When the user drags regular pieces to form an exactly four-piece horizontal line, an exactly four-piece vertical line, a straight line of at least five pieces, or intersecting horizontal and vertical matches, the old pieces that form the pattern finish clearing first. Only after the old icons and their in-cell fade layers have fully exited does the corresponding special piece begin scaling into view: the column-clearing special appears in the middle of the four-piece horizontal line, the row-clearing special appears in the middle of the four-piece vertical line, the color-clearing special appears in the middle of the line of at least five, and the area-blast special appears at the shared intersection. A newly created special piece occupies a definite bookshelf cell and continuously displays a rotating highlight and a gentle sway; the outgoing and incoming visuals are never shown together in the same cell during the handoff. After any later fall, it retains the corresponding appearance and interactive state, and tapping or dragging it starts that special type's ability sequence.

### R8 - Special-Ability Areas and Chaining
When the user taps a special piece or makes an adjacent swap containing one, the triggering icon first clears from its cell. The column-clearing ability covers its entire column, the row-clearing ability covers its entire row, the area ability expands from its center through a diamond-shaped neighborhood, and the color ability clears the most numerous regular-piece type currently on the board. If an affected area contains other special pieces, each special piece cleared in this way continues the chain by activating its own ability. After every affected position has finished clearing, gravity-driven falling, refilling from the top, and automatic cascades proceed. The sequence ends with a full, settled board whose pieces can be dragged again.

### R9 - Circular Bursts and Cauldron Feedback
When the user creates a regular match by dragging, or taps a special piece to activate an ability that clears pieces, each cleared position first emits a brief burst made of 12 uniform, light-colored circular bubble particles. The particles expand outward from near the icon's center while fading. Every regular piece then leaves a same-icon afterimage that shrinks as it follows an arcing path to the cauldron. The circular form remains consistent across basic matches, special clears, automatic cascades, and the end-of-game board explosion. When an afterimage arrives, it disappears while a bubble sound, cauldron sway, and droplets occur in sync. Special pieces use their own sound effect and shake, and repeated dense clears leave no particles or flying objects from a previous round once they finish.

### R10 - Scoring, Combos, and the Final Countdown
As the user repeatedly drags to create matches and automatic cascades, the score at the top continues to increase according to line length, the number of match groups in the same round, the combo round, and how each piece was cleared. Each directly cleared piece is worth 1 point, and each piece cleared as a secondary effect of a special ability is worth 3 points. Accompanied by point sound effects, the displayed number gradually catches up with the accumulated score for the current game. Later cascade rounds show "Combo!" and the corresponding multiplier, and the labels retract when resolution finishes. Once the remaining time enters the final 10 seconds, it flashes red and white; during the final 5 seconds, an outlined number overlays the board second by second. Pausing and resuming must not separate the time, score, multiplier, or grid from the state of the same game.

### R11 - Pausing, Loss of Focus, and End-of-Game Resolution
When the user pauses a round or sends the page to the background, the board stops accepting new gestures; the timer, piece movement, and current resolution sequence stop; and the overlay, background blur, and “Paused” panel appear. Returning to the foreground still requires the user to press “Resume,” and saved progress resumes only after the panel exits. Whether the pause began while the board was settled or during clearing, falling, or another active sequence, resuming must allow both the current sequence and every later match formed by a legal move to proceed through clearing, scoring, falling, refill, and cascades. When the timer reaches zero, new input is disabled immediately. If resolution is still active, “FINISHING” remains visible until all actions finish, followed by the outward board burst, the “Time’s up!” expansion, and the results reveal. The current result is recorded only once across this lifecycle.

### R12 - Results, Replay, and Viewport Reflow
When the user enters the results screen and waits for its entrance sequence, the dragon, panel, three star positions, current difficulty, incrementing score, performance message, settings control, and "Play again" appear in order. A best-score state is shown only when the current game earned a positive score, and "NEW BEST SCORE!" appears when that score reaches the saved best score. Pressing replay then creates a new game using the currently saved difficulty. When the user resizes the browser to a narrow viewport and then restores it, the internal design space of at least 375 by 700 scales uniformly to the actual visible area. The board, cauldron, corner controls, popup, and bottom button reflow together; bookshelf cells remain square, the page does not scroll, and the current score, time, difficulty, results state, or pause state is not reset by the size change.
