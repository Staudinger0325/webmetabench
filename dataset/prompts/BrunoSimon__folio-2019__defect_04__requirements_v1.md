Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Entering the Scene and Resource Readiness
When the user opens the page and waits for the resources to finish preparing, the full-viewport canvas first presents the START marker, driving instructions, and the entrance to the low-poly scene, then allows the car to accept continuous driving input. The visible models, collision boundaries, ground textures, audio, and interaction labels must all belong to the same ready state. Before loading is complete, the car must not pass through parts of the scene that have not been established. Returning to the page after loading is complete must not create the car, wheels, or scene props again, and the first keypress after loading must drive the car directly rather than restart the launch sequence.

### R2 - Desktop and Touch Driving Mappings
While the user holds an arrow key or W, A, S, or D, the car continuously applies drive force and steering in the corresponding direction; Space or Control applies the brakes, and Shift applies a boost. Releasing any key ends only that action, with no lingering input from the previous direction. Touch devices display a virtual joystick and driving buttons; when the finger leaves the joystick, it returns to center and stops producing input. Touch and keyboard input must produce the same results for the chassis, wheels, lights, and engine state. Rapidly alternating between forward and reverse must likewise obey only the input that is still held.

### R3 - Follow Camera and View Input
As the user drives the car, scrolls to zoom, and drags the view, the camera target continuously follows the chassis position, while the zoom distance and pan offset change progressively in response to input. Turning the car or crossing between scene regions must not make the camera jump to unrelated world coordinates. When forward or reverse movement begins again, manual panning returns to vehicle-centered framing. On entering the project-board area, the reading angle smoothly takes over; after leaving the area, the driving angle returns. Reversing back across the boundary must not cause repeated jumping between the two angles, and the car must remain within an understandable follow view throughout.

### R4 - Continuous World and Regional Navigation
As the user drives from START into the crossroads and travels back and forth among the projects area, information area, and playground, the roads, ground graphics, directional cues, static structures, and collision boundaries form one continuous drivable world. Forward travel, reversing, and detours must all allow the user to return to previously visited areas. A region transition changes only the currently visible content and interaction focus; it does not rebuild the entire page. Props that have already been displaced, the camera distance, and the car state must persist during return trips. After the car leaves an area, its old prompt must not continue accepting remote interaction, and returning must not duplicate scene content.

### R5 - Project Boards and External Destinations
When the user drives the car into the interaction area in front of a project board and performs the entry action, that board's image, ground marker, award displays, and external destination must remain associated with the same project entry, and the destination bound to that entry opens in a new tab. When the car proceeds to the next board or reverses out of the projects area, the previous entry's interaction focus and reading camera angle are withdrawn according to the car's position. On returning to the portfolio, the car world remains in the state in which it was left. Re-entering the same board must still open the same destination without duplicating scene models, and the entry prompt must apply only to the current board.

### R6 - Car Physics Reset
If the user releases the driving keys and presses R while high-speed turning, reversing, airborne motion, or a collision is still in progress, the car must establish a new spawn state at the starting position and orientation. The chassis's linear velocity, angular velocity, steering, drive force, braking, wheel suspension, and contact state must all be cleared at the same time. After the reset, the car remains stationary while waiting for new input; it must not continue sliding along its previous momentum, twisting, bouncing, or causing a second displacement of the wheels. Pressing R repeatedly must produce the same stationary result, and the camera and engine sound must immediately change to match the spawn state.

### R7 - Collision Objects and Playground Resets
When the user drives the car into brick walls, the bowling ball, bowling pins, trophies, and pushable props, each object produces continuous displacement, rotation, rebound, and corresponding sounds according to the direction and speed of contact. After coming to rest, the objects can sleep and must resume simulation when contacted again. When the user enters the respective RESET area for the brick walls or bowling setup and performs the interaction, only that group of objects returns to its positions and orientations with zero velocity. The car and the other setup must remain unchanged, and no duplicate objects may be created. After repeated resets, every visible model must still correspond one-to-one with a collider.

### R8 - Car Visual and Audio Synchronization
As the user accelerates, steers, brakes, reverses, and collides, all four visible wheels follow the positions and rotations of the physical wheels frame by frame. The brake lights appear according to the braking state, the reversing lights appear according to the car's actual direction of travel, and the engine pitch and volume change continuously with speed and acceleration. After the car stops or resets, the lights and engine sound return to the corresponding stationary state. Collision sounds must be triggered only by actual contact and must not continue layering with actions that have already ended. After the page returns from the background, sound must resynchronize with the car's current state, and the visible car must not jump away from its position.

### R9 - Roof-Antenna Spring State
When the user makes the car accelerate, turn sharply, or strike an object, the roof antenna deflects opposite to the chassis acceleration, then gradually decays around its upright neutral pose under pullback force and damping. After the chassis rotates, the swing direction must still be transformed into the car's local coordinates. If R is pressed while the antenna is still swinging, its position, rotation, accumulated displacement, and spring velocity must all be cleared together. It must remain neutral on the next frame and must not return to center only to whip out again along its pre-reset direction. Afterward, only new car motion may excite another swing; the antenna must not lean on its own while the car waits at rest.

### R10 - Information Entries and Dialog Layer
When the user drives the car into a social, code-hosting, professional-network, or email entry in the information area and performs the interaction, only the destination bound to the current entry opens. The prompt withdraws after the car leaves the area, and adjacent entries must not respond at the same time. On returning to the portfolio, the car, camera, and previously displaced props retain their state. The course-promotion dialog presents its character, speech bubbles, and answers in message order, and the external course destination opens in a separate page. Closing or advancing the dialog must leave the driving canvas unobstructed, and repeatedly entering the information area must not stack multiple dialog layers.

### R11 - Viewport Changes and Touch Layout
When the user resizes the window, changes the device orientation, or first interacts on a touch device, the canvas, perspective projection, post-processing resolution, virtual-joystick center, and driving-button hit areas must all update for the current viewport. Three-dimensional objects and labels must not be clipped or displaced according to the old dimensions. Using multiple touches to adjust the camera distance must not trigger a driving action at the same time. Changing the input mode adjusts only the presentation of the controls and the rendering load; it does not change the world content, collision rules, or entry destinations. After the device has been rotated repeatedly, the control center must still correspond to its current visible position.

### R12 - Page Visibility and Audio Lifecycle
When the user presses M to toggle mute, sends the page to the background, and then returns, all audible output stops immediately while the page is in the background. On return, audio resumes according to the mute state the user selected before leaving. At the same time, all sustained actions, including forward, reverse, steering, braking, and boosting, return to their released state. Once the page is visible again, the car must not move on its own, audio must not be recreated or layered, and the engine sound must resynchronize from the car's current state, while the scene and visit progress remain unchanged. Repeatedly switching tabs must follow the same lifecycle.
