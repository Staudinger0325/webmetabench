Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loading and Initial View Setup
When the user opens the standard address in a desktop browser, the resource-preparation phase first shows a full-viewport loading screen with a rotating cube, an expanding and contracting shadow, and the text “Loading...”. Once preparation is complete, the loading screen exits and the page establishes only one full-screen 3D canvas, the menu and music controls in the upper-right corner, and the About content layer. The initial window shows the colorful Jafar Rezaei name, an introduction, and the call-to-action button. In the completed state, the character sits typing at a desk in the low-poly office, with no overlap between the window and the scene. Reloading the standard address starts the scrolling narrative again from About, while the saved music on/off state is reflected only in its corresponding control.

### R2 - Office Object Responses
When the user clicks the chicken, floor lamp, and air conditioner on the initial About view, the chicken briefly hops upward with its corresponding sound and then lands back in its original position; each click on the floor lamp switches it between warm-orange and bright-yellow materials; and the air conditioner starts its fan blades and sound, while a second click sends the sound into its ending segment and stops the blades when that segment finishes. The scene clock simultaneously reflects the current local time. Each click updates only the operated object’s associated visible position, material, movement, or sound. The monitor messages and typing cursor continue appearing and disappearing at their respective rhythms, the character and introduction window retain the current composition, and object interactions do not change the main scroll position.

### R3 - Scrolling Narrative and Character Poses
When the user clicks “Up for a magical twist?” or continuously scrolls down from the initial view, the main container advances from About to Skills, the 2D introduction leaves the viewport, and the 3D character transitions from typing into a front-facing fall. From the start of that fall and throughout the transition interval between the falling and showcase actions, the character’s own left arm remains straight on the right side of the screen, with the shoulder, elbow, and wrist forming one continuous long line; once the transition is complete, the character enters the showcase pose. As scrolling continues, the character switches to running, whispering a hint, and finally making a phone call. Continuous upward reverse scrolling restores the character and model positions, selected action, and section visibility for the earlier progress. Adjacent actions affect the pose together only during their fade transition, and the background returns to white after the city section is exited.

### R4 - Menu, Audio, and Cursor
When the user clicks the menu in the upper-right corner of the initial desktop view, the sidebar opens and displays About, Skills, Books, Resume Game, Quick look, and the social links. The menu button moves to the sidebar’s outer edge, and the 3D view makes room for the sidebar. Selecting any of the four content sections smoothly scrolls to its corresponding position; clicking outside the sidebar or clicking the button again closes it and restores the main composition. The global music button switches between playing and muted icons, and the selection persists across refreshes. The soundtracks for About, later content, and the running game fade in and out according to the current state. The white difference-blend cursor follows the pointer, enlarges over hover-responsive controls, and returns to its normal size when the pointer leaves.

### R5 - Skill Badges and Speech
After the user scrolls to Skills, the section shows the Front/Mobile, Backend, Infrastructure, and AI category badges, multiple groups of wrapping technology labels, and colorful blurred geometric decorations behind the content. Hovering over or clicking any technology badge makes the browser read that badge’s label aloud from the beginning, while the 3D character’s mouth moves for the duration of the speech. When the pointer leaves the current badge, the current speech is canceled and the speaking state is cleared. Moving directly onto another badge first cancels the preceding speech and then reads the new label. If an externally hosted badge image fails to load, its alternative text still identifies the corresponding skill.

### R6 - Book Display and External Links
When the user enters Books, “Javascript fundamentals,” “React.js top question/answers,” and the translation-related “Javascript top question/answers” content are arranged horizontally as three-dimensional book cards with covers, spines, page edges, and shadows. Each title and subtitle appears below its own card. When a card is hovered, only the current book rotates slightly around its vertical axis from its initial perspective angle; it returns to that angle when the pointer leaves, while the other cards remain in place. When horizontal space is insufficient, the book list itself handles scrolling. Clicking a book card opens its corresponding address in a new tab, and returning to the portfolio preserves the original page and its Books scroll position. A wide desktop layout shows the GitHub prompt card at the end, while narrower layouts retain the core book cards.

### R7 - Quick look Résumé Reading
When the user clicks the About window or Quick look in the sidebar, a white full-screen reading overlay covers the canvas and presents Work experience, Talks, Certifications, Educations, and Projects in that order. Applicable lists scroll horizontally within their own containers, and the close button in the upper-right corner remains visible during vertical reading. Scrolling inside the reading overlay does not move the main container, and opening it does not separately reset the main scroll position, character, music, or an already-started game. Closing it reveals those objects again in their current states. Entry titles, dates, descriptions, tags, images, Present durations, and addresses use the current résumé data; dynamic durations and counts are not fixed to specific values for a particular visit.

### R8 - Resume Game Runtime Rules
When the user presses Space or clicks the start button while Resume Game has not started, the page moves to the game section and enters the timed, scoring state. Space fires an arrow; W or the Up Arrow makes the player jump; D, the Right Arrow, A, or the Left Arrow makes the player block; S or the Down Arrow makes the player crouch; and Escape pauses the game. Flames, ghosts, and birds advance according to their respective defense rules, and hit outcomes update health, actions, sounds, and score together. While paused, current enemy attacks, flying arrows, the timer, and score freeze, and resuming continues from the visible situation. Clicking Restart clears health, score, time, player action, enemy type, every active arrow, current enemy attacks, and reward visibility in one reset; after the not-started interface is established, no projectile from the previous run continues flying or produces a hit. Victory and defeat both prompt the user to press Space to enter play again.

### R9 - Amsterdam Scene Path
As the user continuously scrolls down from Resume Game, passes through the connecting section, and enters Contact, the colorful Amsterdam streetscape approaches from the distance along a continuous path through depth. From the user’s viewpoint, the scene always advances along a route above and slightly to the right of the river surface, stays on the same side of the waterway, and does not cross the waterway’s centerline. The same row of waterfront buildings, the riverbank, the bridge, and the water retain traceable relative relationships throughout the movement. The streetscape’s movement, enlargement, and rotation are driven in sync by the same scroll progress, ending in a fixed composition of the bridge, character, canal, contact sign, and social links. During continuous reverse scrolling, the scene shrinks and recedes along the same visual route. After the city section is exited, the light-blue background returns to white.

### R10 - Rewards and Résumé Data Synchronization
When the user increases the score in the game and reaches the current reward milestone, the game pauses and shows the corresponding résumé panel from Education, Projects, Certifications, Talks, or Experiences. The panel presents the current category title, an item count calculated from that category’s data, a horizontal or vertical list, images, tags, and external links. Enemies, arrows, timing, and scoring resume only after the user continues. After automatic reward display is disabled, later milestones no longer open an overlay over the game automatically, while the current run continues to progress. Clicking the “Show” control opens the next category currently named in the button text. Entries in that panel remain consistent with the same-named Quick look entries in their titles, descriptions, images, tags, and addresses.

### R11 - Completed Contact View and Return Navigation
When the user scrolls to the bottom of the page, Contact simultaneously presents the Amsterdam canal streetscape, the character near the bridge, the contact sign, the LinkedIn, GitHub, Stack Overflow, and Mail controls, and the credits for model, icon, and sound assets. Web links open in new tabs, Mail uses an email-address link, and the green rounded controls provide scale feedback when hovered. After visiting an external link and returning, the original portfolio page remains available. Alternatively, when the user opens the menu at the bottom and selects an earlier section, the main scroll position updates to the selected destination, the character and city leave the final composition during the continuous reverse scroll, and the Contact controls and credits leave the viewport with their own section.

### R12 - Device Modes and Re-entry
When the user opens the standard address with a desktop user agent, the completed state presents a single full-screen 3D canvas, continuous scrolling content, a fixed menu, and Resume Game. When the same address is opened with a mobile user agent, the completed state does not show the canvas; instead, it shows the mobile-not-supported message and the “Light weight mode” button, which opens the reading mode with the lightweight parameter when clicked. The final lightweight mode shows neither the 3D canvas nor the game and directly provides About, Work experience, Talks, Certifications, Educations, Projects, and Contact. Refreshing that address retains reading mode, while returning to the standard desktop address rebuilds the canvas and scrolling narrative from About.
