Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Fixed Navigation and Direct Section Access
When the user opens the page in a wide viewport and clicks Home, About, Pricing, Features, and Testimonials, the translucent navigation fixed to the top edge of the viewport keeps the two-color dots, horizontal navigation links, and Reach Out visible. The page scrolls smoothly to the corresponding anchor, the fixed bar does not obscure the target section heading, and the content order after navigation matches the order encountered during continuous downward browsing. On a narrow viewport, after the user clicks the menu icon, the vertical navigation links and Reach Out expand below the fixed bar and the icon changes to its close state. Selecting a link or clicking the close icon again collapses the panel while preserving the current scroll position, and the menu, logo strip, and content cards do not make the page overflow horizontally.

### R2 - Hero Content and Reversible Hover States
When the user enters the Hero and waits for its initial presentation, the Use Your Mind. Or Don't. badge, main heading, description, Enter Email.. input group, and main image on the right enter from their respective directions, moving from offset and transparent states into their final two-column positions. On a narrow viewport, they instead stack with the text before the image, and readable space always remains below the fixed navigation. When the user moves the pointer over the star badge, arrow button, or main image, only the current object changes its applicable color, icon displacement, scale, and rotation states. When the pointer leaves, the object returns to its idle pose, while the current text in the email field, lower-page anchors, and the states of other sections remain unchanged.

### R3 - Directional Entrances for Content Sections
As the user continues scrolling from the logo strip through About, How can we help?, Schedule, and Monitor, the section headings, descriptions, steps, stats graphic, and monitoring graphic gradually move from their designated lateral or below-start positions while becoming more opaque. After the entrances complete, they align with the side-by-side or vertical layout for the current width, and each image remains grouped with its own label and description. When the user scrolls in reverse, re-enters these areas after pausing, or changes the viewport width, each display state completes its transition according to the currently visible range. It does not duplicate the same heading, swap the stats and monitoring images, or carry an offset, color, or button state from the preceding section into the next one.

### R4 - Seamless Logo Track Loop
When the user scrolls to Showcased Logos are here. and keeps watching, the first set of Slack, Amazon, MeUndies, SitePoint, and WooCommerce logo images and the immediately following duplicate in the same order move left at a constant speed. The title block stays at the front of the track; when the preceding set leaves the clipped region, the following set immediately occupies the corresponding positions, and the loop resets at a visually equivalent handoff point. As the user waits through multiple loops and hovers over the logo images one by one, the track never shows a completely blank stretch, bounces backward, changes speed abruptly, or makes a visible jump cut. The current image returns from grayscale and reduced opacity to full color and opacity, then reverts when the pointer leaves; hovering over one image does not pause the continuous motion.

### R5 - Pricing and Service Card States
When the user enters Pricing and drags the Team count range control, the visible team count, Starter price, and Enterprise price refresh synchronously from the same thumb state. The thumb remains constrained at either endpoint, and dragging it in the opposite direction continues updating from the most recent input value. Both plan cards always retain their own titles, description lists, color identities, and per-user, per-month pricing meaning. When the user then enters Features and hovers in turn over the Web Design, Ad-Creatives, Automation, and Infographics cards, the current card enlarges slightly, its border becomes subtler, and its shadow increases; when the pointer leaves, it returns to its default size. The pricing state does not rewrite the service set, and a width change only reflows the cards without breaking apart each icon, title, description, and learn more association.

### R6 - Testimonial Carousel and Boundary Feedback
After the user enters Testimonials and clicks the circular left and right arrows or swipes on a touch-enabled device, the carousel moves to adjacent content in the current testimonial set. Every card continuously preserves the association among its avatar, star rating, name and identity, and testimonial text. A narrow viewport reduces the number of simultaneously visible cards, while a wider viewport increases the visible range, and the current content remains fully inside the carousel container. When the user clicks repeatedly until reaching an available boundary, the button for that direction becomes low-opacity and cannot advance farther; operating in the opposite direction restores it to an available state. Repeated switching, viewport changes, and leaving and returning must not mismatch avatars and text, duplicate testimonials, or allow adjacent cards to cover the heading or navigation buttons.

### R7 - Multi-Directional Entrance of the Subscription Card
When the user scrolls down until the blue Subscribe. But why? subscription card enters the viewport, the outer container rises slightly from below and gradually appears, the heading and description enter from one side, and the Enter Email.. input group and dark-blue slanted decoration visible on wide viewports enter from the opposite side. Every object ultimately reaches its designated layout position within the rounded card, with the changes in position and opacity perceptible at the same time. When the user leaves and re-enters, the decoration is hidden on a narrow viewport, or the user types in the input field, the card still retains a clearly directional entrance instead of a single fade at a fixed position. The completed state has no residual offset, and the input value and button hover color remain local to the subscription section.

### R8 - Isolation of Input and Local State
When the user enters different text in the Enter Email.. fields in the Hero and subscription card, then drags the pricing control, opens the narrow-screen menu, and switches testimonials, both email fields retain their own current values. Pricing updates only Team count and the plan prices, the menu controls only the navigation panel, and the carousel advances only the testimonial set; no one operation clears or resets another object. When the user repeatedly focuses and edits the fields, closes the menu, drags in the opposite direction, and returns to previously viewed sections, each local state remains associated with its original control. The arrow buttons show only their existing hover behavior and do not display any page-undefined state for successful sending, account creation, or completion of an external process.

### R9 - Responsive Content Reflow
While the user browses in a wide viewport, the Hero, Schedule, Monitor, plan cards, Features, and subscription card use their respective two-column or grid structures, the partnership steps are arranged horizontally, and the testimonial section uses the wider space to show more current cards. After the viewport is narrowed, the horizontal navigation collapses into a menu, image-and-text groups and cards stack in their original reading order, the subscription decoration is hidden, and the input group remains inside its container. When the user changes the viewport with existing email text, a price value, and a current testimonial, the page only recalculates column counts, spacing, image sizes, and the visible-card range without overwriting those states. The fixed bar does not obscure headings, the logo track remains clipped by its own container, and no image, button, card, or input field causes horizontal overflow.

### R10 - Footer Content and Asset Lifecycle
When the user continues browsing to the footer, the light amber area presents, in sequence, the two-color dots, company tagline, social icons, Company and Get Help link groups, separator, and Copyright Nirdhum with the current year, while the footer content moves from its entrance state into its layout position. On a narrow viewport, the link groups consolidate into a vertical reading structure, while the icons, labels, and copyright line remain independently distinguishable. When the user refreshes the page or waits for the local images and remote testimonial avatars to load, the current year updates according to the access date, and the Hero, logo, stats, and monitoring images remain in their respective containers. An avatar that has not finished loading does not remove its associated name, star rating, or testimonial text, and completed resource loading does not change the anchor order or cover an adjacent section.

### R11 - Subscription Card Entrance Direction and Phase
When the blue “Subscribe. But why?” card first enters view at desktop width, the dark-blue angled background and the “Enter Email..” input group on the right must slide in from offset positions on the same side, move in the same direction, and fade in at synchronized progress. Their horizontal relationship must converge continuously throughout the entrance. At completion they reach the final layout together, while the heading, description, input value, focus behavior, and subscription button remain visible and usable.
