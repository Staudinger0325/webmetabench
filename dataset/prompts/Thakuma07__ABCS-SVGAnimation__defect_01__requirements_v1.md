Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Initial Hero and Fixed Content
When the user opens or refreshes the page, it must present only a single hero that fills the current small viewport height, initially showing the deep-gray foreground, the large uppercase “DIG IN” headline centered along the bottom, and the fixed browser-tab title. The page must not contain navigation, forms, progress controls, a second screen, or any pale-green area revealed in advance. At this point, the reveal mask must be empty, and the central statement must remain on the background layer beneath the foreground. Attempting to scroll to the bottom must leave the page at the start of the same hero, with no blank space around it or additional scrolling range caused by the content height.

### R2 - Dual-Layer Copy and Typography
After the user opens the hero on a wide or narrow screen and the font asset is ready, both the foreground headline and the background statement must use uppercase Hanson Bold letterforms. The headline must remain near the bottom, the statement must remain centered on the pale-green layer, and the two layers must retain identical padding and align precisely across the entire viewport. The responsive layout must scale each text block within its own font-size range, preserving the deep-gray and off-white foreground and the pale-green and dark-gray background. Dragging across the text must neither produce a text-selection highlight nor alter either fixed text block; after wrapping, each block must remain centered without colliding with the viewport edges.

### R3 - Mouse-Trail Coordinate Mapping
When the user first moves the mouse into the hero and then moves continuously along straight, curved, and reversing paths, the initial coordinate must establish the tracking origin. Subsequent movement must create a smoothly following reveal trail in the same viewport region traversed by the pointer, locally exposing the pale-green background layer and parts of the central statement through the deep-gray foreground. The trail must not have a fixed offset from the pointer, reverse either coordinate axis, or drift in scale as the page dimensions change. The transparent reveal layer must not intercept movement input intended for the hero, and marks near an edge must be clipped by the hero boundary.

### R4 - Speed-Based Width and Liquid Blending
When the user first moves the mouse slowly, then quickly crosses a distance and changes direction, the distance between the smoothed position and the target position must map continuously to the width of each smudge mark. Slow movement must leave a narrower window and fast movement a wider one, without abruptly switching to a uniform brush size during acceleration or deceleration. During animation, adjacent marks must blend with softened, thresholded edges into a continuous ink-like trail, and crossing trails must merge into an organic region rather than a series of separate circles. Only areas covered by the trail may expose the pale-green background layer, and sharp turns must remain continuously covered by neighboring marks without splitting into blank gaps.

### R5 - Consistent Touch Dragging
When the user presses and continuously drags on the hero on a touchscreen, the touch-start position must establish the tracking position, and subsequent movement must generate a reveal trail using the same coordinates, smooth following, speed-to-width mapping, and liquid blending rules as the mouse. Fast and slow gestures must produce continuous windows of different widths. During the drag, the page must remain on the current hero without scrolling or selecting text. After the user lifts the finger, no new target positions may be accepted, while the existing catch-up, expansion, and contraction animations continue to completion according to their individual lifecycles.

### R6 - Trail Expansion and Automatic Restoration
After the user creates a trail by moving the mouse or dragging a finger, stops providing input, and waits, each smudge mark must expand smoothly from its speed-based initial width, reach twice its initial radius within 2 seconds, and begin a 3-second contraction at the 2-second point after its creation. Marks created at different times must continue evolving according to their own timelines. Once a mark contracts to zero, it must leave the mask, so earlier regions restore the deep-gray foreground first. After the final mark finishes, the entire pale-green trail must close and the page must return to showing only the deep-gray foreground and “DIG IN”; the previous path must not remain as a permanent painted layer.

### R7 - Overlapping and Repeated Trails
If the user moves again, reverses direction, or crosses the same position while an earlier trail is still animating, each new smudge mark must begin its own expansion and contraction from the time of the new pass, while the earlier marks continue on their original timelines. Both sets of trails must appear in parallel without clearing, restarting, or uniformly extending one another. An overlapping region must keep the background layer visible while at least one active mark still covers it and restore the foreground only after all relevant marks have finished. After a complete restoration, moving again must use the same colors, width mapping, blended edges, and lifecycle as the first interaction.

### R8 - Viewport Resizing and Coordinate Synchronization
When the user resizes a desktop window, changes a mobile device's orientation, or changes the available viewport height, the hero, foreground and background layers, and transparent SVG must update in sync to the new inner width and height. The headline and statement must reflow according to their respective clamp-based font-size rules, while the padding on all sides and the vertical alignment relationships remain intact. Once the responsive update is complete, continued pointer movement must place the trail at the new viewport coordinates, with no stretching or misalignment between the mask and content layers. The page height must still equal the current hero without adding a scrolling region, and trails at the edges must be clipped to the new boundary.

### R9 - Input Boundaries and Hero Constraints
When the user turns the mouse wheel, performs an ordinary click, presses a keyboard key, or attempts to drag-select text on either layer, the page must not enter another mode or alter the fixed copy, colors, or current mark timelines. Only mouse movement and touch dragging within the hero may update the target position and generate trails. These input boundaries must preserve a non-scrollable single-page state, nonselectable text, and a transparent SVG that does not capture input hit testing. After movement stops, the smoothed position may only catch up to the final target and must stop adding new marks once its movement falls below the threshold.

### R10 - Loading and Session Reset
When the user first opens the page and waits for its static assets to load, the page must establish the fixed browser-tab title, Hanson Bold font, dual-layer hero, and empty reveal mask without depending on user data or a remote content list. It must not insert a data-loading panel, empty-content message, or completion message. After the user creates any number of temporary trails, refreshing or reopening the page must clear every mark from the previous session and restart the lifecycle from an empty mask. The colors, copy, layout, and mouse and touch rules must match the starting state of a new session.
