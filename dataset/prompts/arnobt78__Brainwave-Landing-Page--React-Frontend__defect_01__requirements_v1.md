Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Fixed navigation and mobile menu
When the user clicks Features, Pricing, How to use, or Roadmap in the desktop header, the page must scroll smoothly to the corresponding section and synchronize the URL fragment. When the menu button is clicked in a narrow viewport, the navigation overlay must expand to cover the space below the header, scrolling of the main content behind it must stop, and New account and Sign in must be available alongside the primary links. After the user clicks the menu button again or selects any link, the navigation overlay must close and release the scroll lock. After repeated opening and closing, and after switching from a narrow viewport to a desktop viewport, the fixed header, horizontal navigation, current anchor, and main-content scroll state must remain consistent with the last valid action.

### R2 - Hero composition and layering
When the user opens the page and waits for the Hero section to enter, the Brainwave value proposition, description, Get started button, robot window, and "AI is generating" status bar must finish fading in and settling into position. A wide desktop viewport must additionally show the tool icon strip, Code generation notification, and partner-logo row. As the user scrolls through the main visual, the peripheral tools and notification must produce a slight layered displacement. After the entrance completes, the robot image, status bar, concentric-ring background, gradient base, text, and button must retain their prescribed stacking relationships. As the viewport narrows, only the peripheral information must be hidden; the main heading, core image, and primary action must remain inside a readable container without causing horizontal page scrolling.

### R3 - Hero pointer parallax
When the user continuously moves the pointer from the center toward different directions within the Hero main visual, the colored orbs distributed around the concentric rings must undergo slight, continuous, eased parallax displacement based on the pointer's position relative to the container. After the pointer stops, the orbs must settle at the corresponding position, and subsequent movement must continue from their current state. This input response must apply only to the colored-orb group; the concentric rings, large background, robot window, heading, and button must remain fixed reference points. The orbs must complete their initial mount reveal before accepting pointer input. When the pointer leaves and re-enters the area, the orbs must not jump or disappear, and scroll-parallax state must not be mixed into their pointer displacement.

### R4 - Per-card hover image reveal
When the user scrolls to Features and moves the pointer into each card in the current list in turn, every card must provide its own hover behavior: that card's hidden image layer must transition from transparent to a low-opacity reveal. After the pointer leaves the card, the same image layer must fade out through the reverse transition, while the title, description, icon, arrow, and Explore more remain above the reveal layer at all times. When the user moves quickly from one card to another, the previous card must clear only its own reveal and the current card must show only its own image, with no reveal persisting across cards. On touch devices without a persistent hover state, all fixed information on every card must remain fully readable.

### R5 - Collaboration capabilities and app ecosystem
When the user scrolls into the collaboration section, the left-side heading, capability list, and Try it now button must fade in from the left, while the right-side description and app ecosystem must fade in from the right. The Figma, Notion, Discord, Slack, Photoshop, Protopie, Framer, and Raindrop icons must be distributed evenly around the outer ring surrounding the central Brainwave mark, with every icon remaining upright. After the entrance completes, the capability descriptions, circular layering, and wide-screen connection curves must hold their final positions; scrolling away and then back must not return them to an invisible starting point. When the viewport narrows, the left and right content must reflow into a vertical reading order, the icons must not overlap the central mark, and the action must remain after the list.

### R6 - Creative service demonstration scenes
When the user scrolls to How to use, the large Smartest AI image card must first present its capability list and "AI is generating" status bar, followed by the Photo editing and Video generation cards entering in reading order. The photo-editing chat bubble, video tool icons, result image, "Video generated!" message, play icon, and progress indicator must each remain within their respective card. After the entrance completes, these media elements and messages must jointly express a fixed demonstration state; reverse scrolling must not swap them, clear them, or queue them again. In a narrow-screen vertical layout, the large-image crop, chat bubble, bottom status bar, and video controls must remain within their respective card boundaries.

### R7 - Pricing data and action consistency
When the user enters Pricing and views the Basic, Premium, and Enterprise cards, each card must simultaneously present the name, description, price presentation, capability list, and action label associated through the current plan data. Activating Get started for a plan with a displayed price, or activating See the full details, must open the pricing details; activating Contact us for Enterprise must open an email contact. After data or viewport changes, the displayed values, plan meaning, and destination must remain associated with the same plan, and no card may use another card's capabilities or action. Both the side-by-side hierarchy on wide screens and the vertical order on narrow screens must follow the current plan order, and neither text nor buttons may be obscured by the star field, sphere, or side lines.

### R8 - Roadmap status synchronization
When the user scrolls to Roadmap, the current roadmap entries must enter with a stagger in data order, and every card must show a date label, status badge, thematic image, title, and description. Done must use a completed-state icon, while In progress must use an in-progress icon; the badge text and icon must come from the same state on the same entry, and Our roadmap must appear after the card group. After the user scrolls back, the cards must remain in their completed entrance state instead of flashing repeatedly. When the data changes, each card's text, image, and status must update together. Neither the staggered two-column desktop layout nor the single-column narrow-screen layout may change the entries' reading order, and the colored borders, grid, and gradient must remain behind the content.

### R9 - Scroll entrances and lifecycle
During the user's first downward scroll, each section must fade in and move upward or settle into place from the left or right when it enters a readable part of the viewport, while cards in the same list must form a brief stagger in the current data order. When the user scrolls rapidly across multiple sections, every section must still be triggered by its own relationship to the viewport; a later section must not prematurely cover the preceding section, and completed content must retain its final opacity and position. During reverse scrolling, repeated passes, viewport adjustments, or a page reload, completed entrances must not replay or flash within one page lifecycle, while a new page lifecycle must restart mounting and entrance behavior. Throughout this process, decorative motion must not affect the fixed navigation, link hit areas, or body-content reading order.

### R10 - Footer and responsive behavior
When the user scrolls to the footer, the page must show a copyright notice for the year of the visit and icon links for Discord, Twitter, Instagram, Telegram, and Facebook. When the pointer enters a social link, only that link's circular background must change, and it must return to its original state when the pointer leaves. Activating an external link must not reset the current reading position on the single-page site, and the fixed header must remain available afterward for returning to any primary anchor. On narrow screens, the copyright notice and social links must be vertically arranged and centered; in wider viewports, they must be positioned on opposite sides. As the viewport width changes continuously, peripheral decorations must adapt to the available space, while the footer hit areas, primary-section order, anchor destinations, and vertical page scrolling must remain unchanged.
