Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loading Entry and Reveal
When the user opens the page and waits for models, textures, sky textures, and audio to load, the loading screen updates its progress bar and resource-type message from actual resource progress. Explore with Music and Explore in Silence appear only after the resources are ready. After either entry is clicked, both buttons stop accepting repeat clicks and a full-screen color reveal begins. When the reveal finishes, the loading screen is removed and the WebGL canvas, work title, and control column become interactive. Explore with Music starts music and ambient sounds; Explore in Silence keeps music off while retaining the complete scene and all seasonal, day/night, and camera states.

### R2 - Scene Objects and Continuous Updates
When the user enters the scene and continues to wait, rotates the view, or moves closer to the campsite, the canvas simultaneously presents the terrain, pond and shoreline, bridge, tent, campfire, rocks, grass and flowers, bushes, and tree canopies. The sky, fog, lighting, wind lines, falling leaves, flames, smoke, and embers change continuously at their own rhythms and remain consistent with the current season and time of day. These visual changes stop when the user leaves the page. On re-entry, there is only one island, one set of control buttons, and one set of environmental effects. After repeated entries and exits or a long stay, object counts, control states, sound layers, and visual layers remain stable, and every interaction continues to correspond to the current view.

### R3 - Camera Input and View Preservation
When the user holds and drags on the canvas, releases, and then zooms with the mouse wheel or touch input, the camera orbits the center of the scene and settles with damping. Zooming changes the viewing distance while panning remains disabled, and the drag direction, target point, camera position, and on-canvas motion stay synchronized throughout continuous input. If the user first establishes a noticeably different view and then clicks the Strike Lightning button while in Rainy, the camera undergoes only a brief, decaying shake around its position at the instant of the trigger and then returns to the view that existed immediately before that trigger. After the viewport is adjusted, the camera projection, canvas resolution, and allowable viewing distance update together.

### R4 - Seasonal State and Weather Lifecycle
When the user clicks Spring, Autumn, Winter, and Rainy in sequence and repeatedly returns to other seasons, the sole highlighted button, vegetation and rock colors, sky and fog, ground and water, falling leaves and wind lines, rain and snow, campfire, and ambient sounds all update in sync from the same seasonal state. Reclicking the current selection leaves both the notification and the scene stable. On entering Rainy, rainfall, raindrop feedback on the water, periodic lightning strikes, and the Strike Lightning button appear together. When the user switches to Spring, Autumn, or Winter, the button hides as Rainy ends, periodic lightning strikes end, and the target season's water, campfire, weather, lighting, and ambient sounds fully take over the current scene.

### R5 - Day/Night Lighting and Fireflies
When the user clicks Day and Night in any season, the sole highlighted time-of-day button stays synchronized with the time-of-day state of the sky, sun or moon ambience, stars, ambient light, tent lamp, campfire light, vegetation, rocks, and fog. Across repeated transitions, every lighting and environmental effect continues to match the current time of day. On entering Night, fireflies blink at different positions on their own rhythms, drift slowly in different directions and at different speeds with fine jitter, and continuously vary their relative spacing and combination of brightness levels in a natural way. The lights hide on returning to Day; on entering Night again, their motion remains independent and continuous.

### R6 - Rainy-Season Water State
When the user switches to Rainy and continues to drag, release, or zoom the camera, the pond simultaneously shows rainfall, white splashes on the water surface, and ripples along the shoreline. The splashes move with the water-surface perspective and remain spatially anchored to shoreline bends, the center of the pond, and its shallow and deep areas. When the user then switches to Winter, Spring, or Autumn and later returns to Rainy, the splashes, ripples, and ice enter or exit according to the current season and share the current water extent and depth relationships. Repeated transitions leave no water effects outside the shoreline, on the ground, or in regions belonging to the preceding season.

### R7 - Strike Lightning Button Feedback
After the user enters Rainy, the Strike Lightning button appears in the right-side control column and remains clickable. When clicked, the button itself highlights, the electric-arc ring at its edge expands outward into a larger glowing ring and quickly fades, and one random lightning strike and platform-supported haptic response are triggered at the same time. After the highlight and glowing ring end, the button naturally returns to its normal clickable state. During consecutive clicks, each complete sequence of button highlighting, ring expansion and fade-out, random lightning strike, and haptic response is clearly presented and ends independently. When the user leaves Rainy, the button and its electric-arc decoration hide together.

### R8 - Composite Lightning-Strike Effects
When the user clicks the Strike Lightning button in Rainy or waits for a periodic lightning strike, every event selects a strike point within the currently visible ground area, generates a blue-cyan lightning arc extending from the air to the ground, and bursts orange-yellow sparks at that same point while playing a nearby thunderclap and driving a brief camera shake. The sparks move outward from the strike point and gradually shrink and fade. The lightning arc, sparks, sound, and camera movement begin and end to the rhythm of the same event. Automatic and manual triggers both present the same complete feedback, and after the event ends, the view returns to a stable scene for the current season and the user's current viewpoint.

### R9 - Vegetation Cutout Silhouettes and Shadows
When the user drags and zooms the daytime view close to tree canopies, bushes, the campsite ground, or the tent, grass blades and tree leaves have irregular cutout silhouettes. The default bush type, regular tree canopies, and birch canopies retain their respective color groups, leaf-cluster edges, and spatial distributions, and their shapes remain clear and coherent at close range. Shadows on the ground and near the tent retain the edge shapes of the corresponding leaf clusters. As the leaves sway in the wind, changes in the shadows' direction, position, and silhouette follow in sync. After the season or time of day changes, leaf colors, visible silhouettes, shadow shapes, and their relationship to the swaying motion continue to correspond.

### R10 - Graphics Settings and Grass Density Synchronization
When the user opens Settings and selects a preset under Graphics Quality or adjusts a Custom option, the selected value, descriptive copy, numeric labels, and saved state stay synchronized, while grass density, campfire particle richness, shadow appearance, and image clarity immediately reflect the currently effective selection. As the Grass Density slider in Custom is dragged from its minimum to its maximum, the number of grass placement candidates sampled increases with the slider value, and the scene's grass density increases continuously and perceptibly. The numeric label, persisted value, and actual visible density remain consistent with one another. Grass density in the preset tiers likewise shows distinguishable differences in ascending order. Options that require the page to reload display Graphics Settings Changed with Reload Now and Later. After the user chooses Later or closes the prompt, the current view retains the applied state; after the user chooses Reload Now, the reopened page matches the saved settings.

### R11 - Music and Ambient-Sound State
When the user chooses Explore with Music, uses the music button to pause and resume, drags Master Volume, and changes the season or time of day, the button icon, track notification, volume label, music playback, and ambient sounds such as birdsong, crickets, owl calls, rain, thunder, campfire, and lake waves all reflect the current audio, season, and time-of-day states in sync. After each change, the sound mix corresponds only to the current environment. When the user chooses Explore in Silence, actively mutes audio, leaves the page, or sends the page to the background, music, ambient sounds, and thunder stop as required by the current state and do not linger, repeat, or stack. On returning, if sound was previously enabled, music resumes and only the ambient sounds for the current season and time of day play. An active mute remains in effect across background and foreground transitions.

### R12 - Responsive Layout and Resource Lifecycle
When the user resizes the window between desktop and narrow-screen layouts, rotates the device, or changes the device pixel ratio, the WebGL canvas immediately adapts to the current viewport. The island's proportions, viewing range, image clarity, and reveal coverage remain stable; the right-side control column, Settings panel, and reload prompt remain fully within the safe margins; and the page remains full-screen with no document scrolling. When the user repeatedly enters, refreshes, or leaves the page, each entry presents only one island and one control column. After the user leaves, scene motion and notifications stop updating and sounds stop playing. On re-entry, each interaction produces a single response, and the composition, clarity, and control positions remain stable and consistent across viewport changes.
