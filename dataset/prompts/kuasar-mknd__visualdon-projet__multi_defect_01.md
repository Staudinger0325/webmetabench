Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Loading and Page Structure
When a user first opens the page, a loading placeholder covering the main content area must remain visible until the primary data, geographic boundaries, and per-capita data have finished loading. Once the data is ready, the header, control panel, annual ranking, interactive globe, and footer source attribution must appear in a split-column layout on wide screens or a single-column layout on narrow screens. The current year must initialize to the latest available year in the dataset, with Total selected by default. If the current data cannot produce a visualization, the affected region must enter a no-data state; it must not derive fabricated rankings, colors, or country values from empty data or continue displaying stale graphics.

### R2 - Year Selection and Playback
When a user drags the year slider or adjusts it with the Left and Right Arrow keys while it has focus, the center label must respond to the input immediately, after which the header year, ranking title and order, bar values, and globe fills must synchronize to the same year. Clicking Play must advance the year one year at a time; clicking again or using the applicable Space shortcut must pause playback. After playback reaches the final year in the current dataset, it must stop automatically on that year and return to the Play state. Repeatedly pausing, resuming, or reaching either endpoint must never move outside the range, jump back to the beginning, or leave any visualization showing the preceding year.

### R3 - Synchronized Metric Switching
When a user switches between Total and Per Capita, the current year and playback progress must remain unchanged, while the ranking data, globe country fills, color legend, hover values, title meaning, and units update together as one state. Total must use MtCO₂, Per Capita must use tCO₂/hab, and every value must come from the corresponding metric in the current dataset. After the user switches repeatedly during playback, the ranking, globe, tooltip card, and legend must still refer to the same year and metric, with no values, units, color scale, or country order left over from the previous metric.

### R4 - Annual Ranking Interaction
For the selected year, the ranking chart must display the ten countries with positive values in descending order for the current metric, keeping every row's country name, gradient bar length, numeric label, and chart title consistent with one another. When the year changes, countries that remain in the ranking must move to their new positions, while countries entering and leaving must do so through continuous transitions. When a user hovers over or focuses a row, the other rows must fade and the current row must gain emphasis; all rows must return to normal on pointer leave or blur. Clicking anywhere in a row, or pressing Enter or Space while it has focus, must open that country's details. After a language switch, country names must change without losing the ranking order, year, or selection.

### R5 - Globe Encoding and Country Tooltip
As a user explores the globe, countries with positive values must be colored along a continuous blue-to-green-to-yellow-to-red scale, while countries with no current value must use a distinct gray. The legend must identify the emission levels, the no-data state, and the unit for the current metric. After the year or metric changes, the country colors, legend range, and ranking must all use the same current data. When a user hovers over or focuses a country, a glowing outline must follow the projected country boundary, and a tooltip card must show the country's name in the current language, its current value, and the correct unit. Pointer leave or blur must clear both the outline and tooltip; clicking or activating the country from the keyboard must open its corresponding details.

### R6 - Free Globe Navigation
When a user holds down on the globe and drags continuously in any direction, the accumulated horizontal and vertical movement must continuously map to the two rotation dimensions, while the ocean, country boundaries, fills, and hover outline are reprojected together. Vertical dragging must be able to pass over the poles, freely flipping the globe upside down and continuing into subsequent orientations, and the final orientation must remain after release. Further wheel, pinch, Plus, or Minus zooming and Arrow-key rotation must continue from the previous orientation and stop at the established zoom bounds. The globe glow, shadow, and hit area must update concentrically with the scale; repeatedly alternating among input methods must neither reset the orientation nor accumulate zoom beyond the bounds.

### R7 - Opening and Closing Country Details
After a user opens country details from a ranking row or a country on the globe, the translucent details layer covering the globe panel must enter from the bottom, its title must show the selected country's name in the current language, focus must move to the Close button, and the main area must use that country's complete historical record. Clicking Close or pressing Escape must move the details layer down and stop it from receiving input. Once closing is complete, focus must return to the entry point that opened it, while the year, metric, language, globe orientation, and most recently displayed country data remain unchanged. When different countries are selected quickly, the title and chart must show only the latest selection, and a name arriving later for an earlier selection must not overwrite the current country.

### R8 - Country Sector Charts
When a user opens country details, the default view must be a bubble chart encoding year, emission value, and sector. After Split by Sector is checked or unchecked, the circles must migrate continuously between a unified distribution and separate horizontal sector bands. Throughout the movement, each circle must maintain collision avoidance based on its own radius, moving around or pushing away from circles it encounters; after arrival, every circle must retain its year, size, color, and sector binding. Hovering over a bubble or sector legend item must provide the corresponding emphasis and value tooltip. Switching to Stacked Chart must display annual stacked areas for the fixed set of sectors and hide the grouping control. Repeatedly switching the grouping state and chart view must not leave behind an old tooltip, position, or highlight state.

### R9 - Bilingual and Keyboard Operation
After a user clicks EN or FR, the header copy, playback and metric labels, ranking title, globe legend and navigation hints, no-data state, detail controls, chart modes, sector names, and available country names must all switch to the selected language, and the page language setting must change with them. The year, metric, playback state, selected country, and chart mode must remain unchanged. Using only the keyboard, a user must also be able to skip to the main content, operate playback and the timeline, focus ranking rows and countries, rotate and zoom the globe, open and close details, and explore the charts. Focus emphasis, accessible names, and accessible states must match the visible feedback provided for mouse interaction.

### R10 - Responsive Behavior and Lifecycle
When a user resizes the window or opens the page on devices of different sizes, the wide-screen split-column layout must become a narrow-screen single column, while the controls, ranking, globe, legend, and detail controls remain readable and operable. The globe must be reprojected around the center of its new container, and the ranking and country charts must be rebuilt for the available width and height, without resetting the current year, metric, country, rotation, zoom, or chart mode. When the user leaves and returns to the page or repeatedly opens and closes details, playback timing, delayed input handling, pending hover work, resize handling, and chart motion must end with the relevant page or detail session, so one input produces exactly one state change. Previously resolved country names must not allow stale content from another language or country to overwrite the current view.

### R11 - Synchronized Globe Layers Across Pole Crossings
When the user continuously holds and drags the interactive globe vertically across either pole and keeps moving, the ocean, country fills, border outlines, and hover outline must always use the same current projection and rotation phase. At the crossing, borders must not continue in the old direction, slide across country surfaces, detach from their fills, and then catch up; hover highlighting and click hit testing must also correspond to the country currently visible under the pointer. All layers must remain synchronized frame by frame when the gesture reverses across the pole again or crosses repeatedly. On release, the final orientation, zoom, and selection behavior must continue directly from the unified projection at that moment without an additional catch-up motion.
