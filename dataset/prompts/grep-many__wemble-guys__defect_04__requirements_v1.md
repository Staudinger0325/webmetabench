Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Room Entry and Lobby Identity
After the user opens the page and completes the room connection, the full-viewport 3D lobby must show a character colored according to the local profile, an overhead name, a green waiting hexagon, the WEMBLE GUYS logo, the current members' avatar-and-name list, the lower-left joystick, the music control, and Jump and Wave. Only the host can initiate the shared countdown by clicking START; other members see the fixed message “Waiting for the host to start the game...”. As members join or leave, the top profile entries and in-scene characters are added or removed to match the current member set, while dynamic names, avatars, colors, and identities remain correctly associated. Scrolling the mouse wheel must not move the page or change the room progression.

### R2 - Round Progression and Restart
After the host clicks START, the central dark overlay becomes transparent and the start control disappears, and the page proceeds through the countdown, active match, winner podium, and return to the lobby in that order. Characters remain in their spawn state until the countdown ends; during the active match, the counter changes to cumulative elapsed time; and at the end, the character associated with the winning profile appears at the same time as the white podium. After the presentation finishes, death states, positions, orientations, hex-tile hit states, and the presentation camera state are cleared with the round. Starting again generates an untriggered arena and valid spawn states, while the current session's music selection remains in effect.

### R3 - Keyboard Movement and Action Transitions
While the user holds W, A, S, D, or an arrow key, the character continuously moves forward or backward along its current facing direction or turns left or right; releasing the corresponding key removes that directional component. The character plays the running animation while it has horizontal velocity and returns to idle when it has neither horizontal nor vertical movement. Position, rotation, and action are synchronized together as one set of member state. When the user presses Enter or F or clicks Wave, a stationary character enters the waving animation; if it then begins moving, jumping, or falling, it switches to the corresponding animation. Using an on-screen control must not clear a keyboard direction that is still held.

### R4 - Joystick Direction and Release State
When the user presses within the lower-left joystick and drags in any direction, the inner circle continuously moves away from the center of the base in the current direction, and the offset direction and magnitude are simultaneously mapped to the character's forward or backward movement and left or right turning. If the user changes the drag angle while maintaining contact, the indicator position and character response immediately follow the new direction. When the user moves the pointer back toward the center or releases the mouse or touch contact, the input magnitude decreases accordingly until it stops, and the inner circle returns precisely to the center. Releasing the joystick clears only the joystick component; it does not affect keyboard directions that are still held or the independent states of Jump and Wave.

### R5 - Landing-Gated Jump
After the character completes a landing on a hexagon that still has a collision surface, pressing the space bar or clicking Jump gives the character only one upward velocity impulse. The character then moves continuously through ascent, the apex, and descent, with its action changing according to vertical velocity between the ascent and falling animations. Repeatedly pressing the space bar or clicking Jump before the character lands again must not reset vertical velocity, interrupt the descent, or create an additional upward thrust. Jump eligibility is restored only after the character contacts an available hex tile again, and the keyboard and on-screen controls share this state.

### R6 - Single Collapse Timeline per Hex Tile
When an active character first lands on an untriggered hex tile, that tile changes from its level's color to orange and begins fading continuously. After briefly retaining its collision support, its visible surface and collision surface disappear together. Each tile progresses independently from its own first hit, and a character that later passes through the empty position continues falling under gravity. If a character genuinely leaves and re-enters the tile, jumps from it and lands on it again, or crosses the same tile repeatedly, the existing opacity progression and remaining lifetime continue without resetting: the tile does not return to fully opaque orange, and its removal time is not postponed. Merely pressing Jump does not establish a new collapse timeline.

### R7 - Multi-Level Falls and Elimination Flow
During an active match, when the user moves to the edge of a hex tile or crosses a position where a tile has been removed, the character falls continuously from the current level. If the character contacts an existing hexagon on a lower level, it regains its landed state and can continue moving and jumping. The staggered tiles across the multiple levels retain their respective base colors, vertical spacing, hit appearances, and collision results. After the character falls out of the arena, its 3D model disappears, its corresponding top avatar turns grayscale, and input no longer changes the character or any hex tile. The local camera turns toward a surviving member, and the podium presentation begins when the surviving-member set reaches the round-ending condition.

### R8 - Member Profiles, Camera, and Shared State
When the user enters a multiplayer room and controls a character, the character color, outlined camera-facing overhead name, top avatar and name, and later podium identity must all reference the same member profile. The local position, rotation, and action are shared with the room, remote characters reproduce the received state, and a hex-tile hit updates only the corresponding position. While the user is alive, the camera follows the local target continuously; after elimination, it turns toward a surviving member. Dragging the canvas changes only the viewing angle, and entering or leaving the podium switches to the presentation or default view respectively. These viewpoint changes must not alter member data, winner data, physical state, or hex-tile data.

### R9 - Music Toggle and Event Sounds
After the user clicks the music control on the right, the off icon changes to its enabled appearance and the background music begins looping; clicking it again pauses the music and restores the off appearance. This selection persists across the lobby, countdown, active match, winner presentation, and next round, and repeated clicks do not change character positions, the member set, hex-tile progression, or the counter. When the user steps on a tile, falls out of the arena, or enters the winner presentation, the corresponding event sound is synchronized with the associated state changes: the tile turning orange, the character disappearing, the avatar turning grayscale, and the podium camera appearing. If the browser does not start audio, the canvas, input, and shared progression must continue.

### R10 - Loading, Viewport, and Scene Lifecycle
When the user opens the page and waits for resources to become ready, the loading progress represents preparation of the character, hexagon, font, and environment resources, and the complete WebGL scene is assembled only after loading finishes. When the user scrolls the mouse wheel or resizes the window, the canvas covers the current viewport and the page does not move vertically; the current room, characters, hex tiles, counter, and music selection are not reset by the resulting reflow. On wide screens, the member list is arranged horizontally at the top; on narrow screens, it moves below the upper-left logo and is arranged vertically. Edge controls remain usable, and names are truncated to fit the available space. Scene transitions clear only the previous round's temporary physical state and hex-tile records.

### R11 - Real-Time Joystick Synchronization During Reversal
While the user keeps holding the inner handle of the mobile circular joystick and quickly drags through the center into the opposite direction, the handle position must correspond to the current pointer on every frame and remain synchronized with the input angle, character heading, and movement direction. The handle must travel through the center along the real gesture and enter the new quadrant immediately; it must not remain in the previous direction, continue along the old direction and then catch up, or lag visually while the character has already turned. The same relationship must hold across repeated horizontal, vertical, or diagonal reversals during one hold. On release, the handle returns from its true current position, and the next hold inherits no direction or lag from the previous gesture.
