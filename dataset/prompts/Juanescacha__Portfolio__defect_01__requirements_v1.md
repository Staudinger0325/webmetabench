Acceptance checklist:
## Public acceptance checklist

### R1 - Fixed Navigation and Narrow-Screen Menu
When the user clicks About, Experience, Projects, or Contact on a wide screen, the fixed navigation bar must smoothly move the page to the corresponding anchor and highlight the most recently selected entry. Clicking the Juanes | Portfolio brand must return to the top of the page while clearing both the highlight and the temporary menu state. After narrowing the viewport, clicking the menu icon must expand the same set of entries below the navigation bar; selecting an entry must collapse the menu and continue scrolling to the anchor. When the menu is opened and closed repeatedly, the user returns to the top after making a selection, or the viewport width is adjusted again, only the navigation layout and clickable areas required by the current viewport may remain.

### R2 - Hero Content and Resource Loading
When the user opens the page and waits for the hero resources, the fixed brand, Hi, I'm Juanes, the developer introduction, the purple guide line, the curved textured background, and the capsule-shaped reading control must all be visible, while the workstation canvas occupies its designated area and displays the model's current loading progress. Clicking the reading control must take the user to About. Once the model has loaded, when the user scrolls away and returns or refreshes the page, the workstation must replace the placeholder without shifting the title or reading control. The mouse wheel must continue moving through the long page and must not zoom the model, while a refresh must restore the initial view under a new resource-loading lifecycle.

### R3 - Service Cards and Perspective Reset
When the user scrolls to Introduction and Overview., the personal introduction and the role cards in the current service list must appear in reading order. As the pointer moves from one side of any card to the other, the gradient border, dark inner panel, icon, and role title must tilt continuously around that card's own center and move slightly forward. After the user leaves the card, moves quickly into an adjacent card, scrolls away, or changes the viewport width, the card that lost the pointer must complete its transition and return to a flat, unrotated state. When entered again, it must calculate its perspective anew from that flat state, and no other card may inherit its angle or enlarged state.

### R4 - Work-History Timeline and One-Time Entrance
When the user continues scrolling from About to What I have done so far and Work Experience., the vertical timeline must present the company icon, role, company, date, and responsibility text in the current work-history order. An entry with no responsibilities must still have its own node and date space and must not collapse into the content before or after it. After the user reads downward through several nodes, scrolls back up, and then uses the navigation bar to enter another section, the nodes and headings that have completed their entrance must remain readable. Their data order and node associations must remain unchanged, and revisiting the section must not return already visible content to transparency or attach another company's icon to it.

### R5 - Workstation Orbit and Fixed Lighting
When the user presses and drags horizontally within the hero workstation canvas, the camera must continuously change the view around the monitor, PC tower, keyboard, mouse, speakers, and desk while remaining within a fixed pitch range. On release, it must stop at the current angle, and the next drag must continue from that view. The scene's main light and fill light must remain anchored in workstation space and must not orbit with the camera. As the view moves from the front to the side or rear, each surface's highlights, shadows, and dark areas must reflect the same fixed lighting direction; dragging back to a previous view must restore the earlier lighting relationship. The lighting must not drift after release, and the initial composition and light-dark distribution must remain consistent after a refresh.

### R6 - Independent Technology-Ball Canvas Input
When the user scrolls to the technology section after the work history and drags any decaled polyhedral ball, only the viewing angle in that ball's own canvas may change. That ball must retain its decal and gentle floating motion, while the other technology balls continue operating independently. On release, the page must remain at its original reading position and allow another drag to continue from the ball's current view. When the pointer is over a ball and the user scrolls, the mouse wheel must still move the page toward Projects without enlarging the ball. If a resource is delayed, its loading feedback may occupy only the corresponding grid cell; once loading finishes, the placeholder must be replaced by the correct decal without changing the arrangement of adjacent balls or the next section.

### R7 - Project-Card Data and Link Boundaries
After the user reaches My Work and Projects., moving the pointer within any project card must make the screenshot, title, description, technology tags, and circular repository entry in the upper-right corner tilt continuously as one card face. Throughout the perspective movement, the screenshot and circular entry must remain separately clickable and open that card's current live-demo target and code repository, respectively. After the user leaves the card, moves quickly to another card, scrolls away, or returns from an external tab, the previous card must return to a flat, unrotated state and the project grid must retain its reading position. When the current data changes, the text, media, tags, and two links on the same card must update together without becoming associated with an adjacent entry.

### R8 - Contact Form and Background Canvases
When the user enters a name, email address, and message in Contact., the three fields must update independently. An invalid email format must display Please enter a valid email; correcting the format must remove the message while preserving the other text. Submitting with incomplete fields must display Please fill in all the fields correctly. and retain the entered content. When all fields are complete and the form is submitted, the button must change from Send to Sending... and remain synchronized with the request state. When the request finishes, the form must leave the sending state and present either a successful thank-you message or a failure message. Meanwhile, the Earth must continue rotating automatically and remain horizontally draggable, while the pink-purple stars stay in the background layer without covering the fields, messages, or button.

### R9 - Responsive Reflow and Session State
When the user changes the viewport from wide to narrow, the full navigation bar must collapse into a menu, the brand must use its shortened display, the hero text and workstation must scale and reposition to avoid overlap, and the service cards, timeline, technology balls, project cards, contact form, and Earth must reflow into a vertical reading order. Returning to a wide viewport must restore the multi-column and side-by-side relationships. Throughout these adjustments, unsubmitted form text, loaded models, and sections that have completed their entrance must retain their current session state, while cards that have lost the pointer must remain flat. After a page refresh, the menu must be closed, no navigation entry may be selected, the form must be empty, and each canvas must begin its own loading lifecycle again.

### R10 - Media Layering and Cross-Section Isolation
As the user scrolls continuously through the hero, introduction, work history, technology, projects, and contact sections, company icons, technology decals, project screenshots, and 3D materials must always appear on their associated objects, with space reserved for text and links both before and after the media loads. Loaded content must remain visible when the user returns to a section already read. When the user drags the computer, a technology ball, or the Earth; hovers over a service card or project card; or opens an external target and returns, the camera, card-face, navigation, and form states of the different sections must not affect one another. Foreground text and controls must remain selectable and clickable, and neither the stars nor the 3D canvases may intercept input intended for other sections.
